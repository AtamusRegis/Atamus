# Atamus: how the game works today

This is a living spec of the game. Read it before every patch, and update it in the same commit as any change to behavior. Rules marked **(owner)** were decided explicitly by the owner, so keep them unless the owner says otherwise.

## The 3D rebuild (owner, 2026-10-12)

The owner wiped the 2D game to rebuild Atamus in 3D. **The whole 2D game — its world, client, UI, modules, combat, ETA, market, pilots, licenses and the rest — lives on the `legacy-2d` branch** (tip `e56e61d`); bring pieces back from there only when the owner asks. The accounts backend (auth, sessions, security, the admin API, the deploy countdown) was kept on `main` and still runs; the admin panel and chat have no frontend right now.

**Old data is kept (owner):** accounts, credits, pilots, licenses and the old game tables stay in the database untouched for reference. The 3D game only reads and writes `users` (auth) and its own `world3d` table.

## The world (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **One shared 3D world on one server: no POIs, no systems, no instances. Everyone sees everyone.**
- A flat ground plane with a **2D grid** (100 m cells) drawn on it.
- **The border is the same flat-top hexagon** (flat edges top and bottom, points east and west), **126 km flat to flat** (owner, 2026-10-12; was 5 miles), drawn as a glowing band on the ground. It stops players: movement walks as far along the step as still fits, and the sphere's edge rests on the line.
- **Each player is one ship** (owner, 2026-10-12: the owner's `Combat_Cruiser.glb`, Y up, +Z forward, in `website/models/`), **scaled to 100 m long**, resting on the grid and **turning to face the way it travels at a fast fixed rate** (owner: "almost instantly, but not instantly"; **4 rad/s**, owner 2026-10-13: 3× slower than the first 12; a U-turn takes about 0.8 s), pivoting about the hull's middle. The model's nose is -Z, flipped on load. **The ship faces the way it flies (WASD), never the reticle** (owner, 2026-10-13; it tracked the circle for a night). While the middle mouse is held, **the circle keeps the pinned screen spot in every case** (owner, 2026-10-12): orbiting sweeps it across the ground with the view, and under WASD it rides at the same screen position; the pointer takes the circle back when the middle button lifts. **With neither right-click nor WASD, it keeps the last direction it faced** (owner, 2026-10-12): only held input re-aims your ship, so network-correction nudges never turn it; other ships re-aim only at a sustained real speed. Everyone flies the same ship for now. One per account; it spawns within 300 m of the center the first time and comes back where it was after that (positions saved every 10 s and on leaving; a saved position outside the hexagon respawns at the center). Until the model loads, a blue sphere stands in. The server still treats players as 50 m circles for the border.
- **Travel is on the ground plane only (XY): no movement up or down on Z.**
- **Speed: 300 m/s** (owner, 2026-10-12; `SPEED` in `world3d.js`). Crossing the map takes about 7 minutes.
- **Weight (owner, 2026-10-13):** ships have momentum. Thrust accelerates at **200 m/s²** toward the input's velocity (0 to 300 m/s in about 1.5 s straight), but **a push off the nose gets only part of it**: full when the nose points where it's pushed, about half side-on, **30% pushing against the nose** (it has to come round first). **Letting go of WASD brakes at 400 m/s²**, twice the acceleration, however the nose points (300 m/s to a stop in about 0.75 s). The nose turns toward the **input** direction, not the drift. Server and client integrate the same way (`ACCEL`, `BRAKE`, `TURN_ACCEL` in world3d.js, sent in the hello); hitting the border still stops the ship dead.
- **The capacitor (owner, 2026-10-13):** a 0–100 bar above the hotbar. **Sprinting drains it** (20 a second), **moving without sprinting recharges it slowly** (4 a second), **sitting still faster** (12 a second). Run dry and sprint stays off until it's back to 15. The server enforces it (each player is sent their own charge 4 times a second, to them only) and the client predicts it the same way.
- **The stop marker (owner, 2026-10-13):** while the ship is moving, a light-blue dashed line runs to a small flat dot where it would come to rest if the player let go now (its velocity at the braking rate). Only the player sees their own.
- **Sprint (owner, 2026-10-12): holding Shift doubles the speed.** The sprint is just a flag to the server (`{t:"move", x, y, b}`); the server applies the doubling. *Open: its touch equivalent.*
- **X drops a cube** (owner, 2026-10-12: a movement reference "for now"): a 50 m cube at the player's position, local to this browser (others don't see it; the newest 200 are kept; gone on reload).
- The grid is drawn by the ground's own shader (anti-aliased 100 m lines on the surface itself), after a separate layer of rendered lines flickered against the ground.
- **Server-authoritative:** the client only sends a direction and its ship's heading (`{t:"move", x, y, b, h}`, direction clamped to length 1, heading wrapped); **after movement or rotation stops, the settled state is sent twice more** (owner, 2026-10-13) so the server and everyone else land on the real final position and heading. Other screens draw each ship with the heading its owner sent (snapshots carry `[id, x, y, heading]`); the server simulates at 20 Hz and sends snapshots at 15 Hz to every client. The client predicts its own sphere locally (with the same border rule) and is softly corrected by snapshots; other ships keep a short history of snapshot positions timed by the server's clock and are drawn 150 ms in the past, interpolated between the two snapshots around that moment (briefly carried on along their last motion if a snapshot is late); they turn only on frames where they really moved.
- A player leaving (socket closed) despawns at once; their position is saved.

## Sides and the minimap (owner, 2026-10-13)

- **Every time a player enters the game they choose a side: Red or Blue** ("Choose a side", two buttons over the world). They join the world only once they've picked; the server keeps the side for the session (`{t:"side", s}`, once per session, only "red" or "blue"). Reconnecting asks again.
- **The minimap:** the map's hexagon at the bottom left, north up. **Friendly ships (your side) are blue dots, enemies red**; your own dot is blue with a white outline and a short white tick for where the camera faces. Redrawn ten times a second.
- Ships themselves look the same for both sides for now.
- PTR only: `game.html?as=Name` connects as another account (for testing several players locally).

## Controls (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **WASD moves the sphere; wherever the camera is facing is "forward".**
- **The middle mouse button, dragged, rotates the camera around the player** — **inverted** (owner, 2026-10-12): dragging right turns the view right. On touch, a one-finger drag does the same (mobile parity: drag = drag). *Open: how movement itself works on touch — nothing is decided yet.*
- **The camera orbits the player looking down toward it** at pitch 60°, zoom 600–1,800 m, rotation 60% (owner: "60d: 600-1800m: 60%"). **The camera sliders are hidden now (owner, 2026-10-12)**, and the hidden settings always use the owner's values above — values a browser saved while the sliders were visible are ignored (owner, 2026-10-13: stale saved zoom caps strangled the aim zoom). Only the Reticle slider's value persists per browser. **The one slider shown (top right) is Reticle** (owner): the aim circle's max distance from the player — the camera leash — 100–3,000 m, default 700, kept per browser. The mouse wheel (touch: pinch) zooms within the range; the right-click leash zooms out at most to the max.
- **Right-click aiming (owner, 2026-10-12, reworked 2026-10-13):** holding the right button draws the **aim line**: a dashed white line from the ship to a **small flat white dot, the reticle** (scaled with zoom). **The reticle is the ground point under the pointer, clamped to the range circle** (radius = the Reticle slider; the circle itself is shown when Assists is on): push past it and the reticle rides its edge.
  - **The camera is driven only by the pointer's screen position and constants**, never by the reticle's ground distance (tying them looped: the zoom moved the ground under the pixel, which moved the zoom). **A dead zone around the screen's center** (owner, 2026-10-13): aiming near the ship neither zooms nor leashes, easing in from 30% to full at 80% of the way to the screen's bound (it used to jitter there, ship and pointer pulling like magnets; full press inside the screen also means maximized windows don't need edge-hugging).
  - **Overflow** (owner's diagram): the gap between the range circle's edge and the camera bounds when aiming toward a direction = the Overflow slider (default 100) plus a fixed per-direction delta (**N S E W +200, diagonals 0**, blended smoothly; the compass sliders that set them were removed). The zoom and the leash (the focus sliding out along the aim) are solved together so that gap comes out exact, with the ship and an 80 m margin kept behind the focus. The view's ground extents are computed exactly per direction from the pitch (the top of the screen sees far more ground than the bottom). Never under the wheel zoom; capped only at 6,000 m.
  - The reticle is **re-projected after the camera takes its final pose each frame**, so it never trails the pointer (aiming is all client-side). Releasing eases everything back.
  - **Middle mouse while aiming:** the camera frame (zoom and the focus's offset) freezes and orbits the player with the yaw; the cursor is pointer-locked at the press spot and the reticle stays raycast from that screen spot through orbits and WASD alike; lifting the middle button frees the cursor.
  - Nothing fires yet: aiming drives the spread display and, for interaction items, the ship's facing.
- The browser's own right-click menu never opens in the game (kept from before).
- **Touch devices (owner, 2026-10-13: "this will be a PC game, just using mobile to test as a second account"):** a **joystick** at the bottom left drives movement like WASD (analog, small dead zone; forward = where the camera faces); **dragging anywhere else turns the camera**, two fingers on the world pinch the zoom. The minimap moves to the top left, and the hotbar and tuning panel are hidden. **Aiming has no touch version, by the owner's decision** (an exception to mobile parity), and there's no sprint on touch.

## The hotbar and aiming items (owner, 2026-10-13)

- **The aim line** is a **dashed white line** from the ship toward the pointer, **ending in a small flat white dot** (the reticle; owner, 2026-10-13); the old blue reticle ring is hidden (owner, 2026-10-13). Dashes and the circle scale with zoom.
- **Assists toggle** (owner, 2026-10-13): a checkbox at the top of the panel turns the **distance (reticle-limit) circle** and the **slot's spread lines** (wedge, arc and fill) on or off; the aim line and its circle always show, and the interaction circle stays. Kept per browser (`atamus.assists`).

- **A hotbar of three slots** at the bottom center: **1 Primary, 2 Secondary, 3 Tertiary**. Keys **1–3** or a click / tap pick the active slot (outlined orange).
- **Each slot's item** has an **aim range** (m), an **aim speed** (degrees of spread closed per second), an **aim spread** (degrees either side of the aim line) and whether it's an **interaction object**. **Moving opens the spread** (owner, 2026-10-13): each item has a **movement spread speed** (degrees per second): while WASD is held the spread opens at that rate instead of closing, **walking up to the yellow point at most** (a third of full spread; above it, it keeps closing down to there), **sprinting twice as fast and up to full spread**; standing still it closes at the aim speed. **The tuning panel shows the selected slot's Range, Speed, Move, Spread and Interact** (owner, 2026-10-13), switching with the slot; values are kept per browser (`atamus.slots`). Placeholder defaults (`SLOTS` in game3d.js): Primary a ranged weapon (600 m, aim 20°/s, move 10°/s, 30°), Secondary a ranged weapon (700 m, 8°/s, 6°/s, 20°), Tertiary an interaction object (the interaction circle's 70 m, 60°/s, 20°/s, 45°).
- **The interaction circle:** a second, green circle around the ship, **20 m out from the hull** (70 m from its center, the ship being 100 m long). An interaction object works on other objects within it. The reticle and camera behave the same for every item.
- **Facing:** while aiming an **interaction object** the ship turns to face the pointer; with a ranged weapon it faces the way WASD pushes it, and **when no WASD is held, aiming any item turns the ship toward the aim point** (owner, 2026-10-13). Not aiming and not moving, it holds its heading.
- **The aim spread** (owner's diagram): while aiming, two lines run from the ship out to the item's **aim range** (not to the reticle), each starting the item's spread off the aim line on either side; an arc at the aim range closes the wedge, with a light transparent fill between. **Holding the aim closes the spread** toward the aim line at the item's aim speed; **swinging the aim reopens it**: a 180° swing fully, 90° by half, 45° by a quarter (proportional). Releasing and aiming again starts wide; switching items mid-aim starts the new item wide. **Color:** red at full spread, orange midway, green as it closes on the aim line. Visual only for now: nothing fires.

## Accounts and sessions

Kept from before, unchanged (`auth.js`, `sessions.js`, `security.js` and the rest): sign-up with the EULA, log in / log out, Remember Password, password recovery and reset requests, device ids, failed-login backoff, impossible-travel locks, bans and the session cookie. **One session per account:** a new tab or device replaces the old one (close 4002: "Playing on another tab or device"). A dead session (4001) goes back to the login page.

- **The website** (atamus.io) is unchanged except play.html: **no more character or pilot creation** — a logged-in account goes straight to **Enter System**. The front page, login, signup, recovery and the EULA are as they were. The pixel logo and icons stay.
- The admin panel's API, moderation, appeals, tickets, the name filter and the trade-watch tables still exist server-side but have no frontend; admin hooks into the game world are disconnected (kicks and freezes don't reach the 3D world yet).

## Live updates (kept)

- Every push to `main` warns every connected player ahead of the switch — **1 s for now (owner, 2026-10-12: "make countdown 1 second for now"**; it was 30 s and goes back up when the owner says): the game shows **"Update incoming — N s"** top center, then sends them to `play.html?updating=1`, which waits for the new build and offers Enter System again. The length lives in `gamehttp.js` (`announceUpdate(part, 1)`) and the `sleep` in both deploy workflows.
- The deploy workflows are unchanged (`deploy-website.yml` on `website/**`, `deploy-server.yml` on `packages/server/**`).
