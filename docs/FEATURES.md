# Atamus: how the game works today

This is a living spec of the game. Read it before every patch, and update it in the same commit as any change to behavior. Rules marked **(owner)** were decided explicitly by the owner, so keep them unless the owner says otherwise.

## Back to basics (owner, 2026-10-10)

The owner took the game back to scratch to focus on the Prospector's gameplay. **The game is the Player, Pilot, Chat and Fleet windows, the world boundary, a Prospector per pilot, and asteroid belts to align and warp to.** Gone from the live game: stations, stargates, planets, the sun, mining, modules, fitting, the market, whispers, settings and music, bases, the tutorial shard and every license in the trainer (Mining Frigate came back on 2026-10-11). **Modules, mining and a temporary Station with a hangar came back on 2026-10-11** (owner; see Modules and mining, The Station). (The Fleet window and Local / Corp chat came back the same day, owner: "I didn't tell you to get rid of the Fleet window". Target locking, the HUD with its hotbar, and the ship inventory came back on 2026-10-11, owner.)

- **Kept for reference:** everything that was live just before is in `reference/2026-10-10-pre-reset/` (client, server, tests, and the full FEATURES.md that described it) and at commit `0a745ec` (the last one before the reset). The art is still in `website/assets/`. Bring things back from there when the owner asks for them.
- docs/DESIGN.md is still the long-term plan.

## The world (owner)

- **The Expanse's hexagon, empty:** 1 AU across at the map scale the game always used (1 AU = 20,000 km), so **20,000 km across, flat to flat**, with flat top and bottom edges. The origin is its center. Its edge is drawn as a thin line.
- **The edge stops ships:** a move order past it is pulled just inside, and a ship that reaches it stops dead there.
- **One Prospector per pilot:** each of your pilots has their own (id `<account>:ship:<pilot>`), which first appears within 5 km of the center. A new pilot gets one (the server rereads your pilots when the client says so, and every minute); a pilot that's gone takes theirs along. It's the only ship (`SHIP_TYPES.chisel` in constants.js): 128 m long, top speed 335 m/s, acceleration 0.15 km/s², hull 200, shield 225 (shown in the Fleet window and the HUD; nothing damages ships yet), **targeting range 5 km, 5 targets, 3 s to lock** (its numbers from before the reset, owner's balance pass), **holds** (owner: every storage type is listed): an **Ore Hold of 5,000 m³** (the 5,000 m³ it starts with) and a **Cargo Hold of 50 m³** (as before the reset), **armor 150** (new with the Fitting window: a placeholder), **4 active and 3 passive module slots** (owner), max power 40 and max disposition 70 (its capacitor and disposition from before the reset). Its modules: Modules and mining.
- **Flying (owner):** the ship flies **straight to its destination until it's given another**, over anything in the way (ships, asteroids, Library props); it accelerates as fast as it can while still able to brake in time, faces the way it's moving, and stops on the point. At top speed, crossing the hexagon takes about 16½ hours.
- **Nothing blocks a ship, but it can't stop on an object (owner, 2026-10-11).** Ships pass over each other and over asteroids and Library props. **A destination can't be set within 70 m of touching an object** (an asteroid out to half its size, a prop its art's whole box; `obstacles.js`): it's moved just outside. Hovering an object (touch: pressing it) shows that zone as a **light dotted outline** (owner). (Pathfinding around objects was tried the same day and taken out, owner.)
- **Logging off** (see Logging off): with every ship docked, your ships leave the world 20 s after the connection closes (a reload keeps them); with one undocked, they keep working for 5 minutes, then safe-warp. Logging back in puts each where it was (pulled inside the edge if it somehow wasn't). Positions are saved every 10 s and on leaving.
- **Other players:** your own ships are always sent to you (with their pilot, hull and shield). Other ships are sent while they're inside your camera's view (the client reports its view; a view wider than 40,000 km is capped). Other players' destinations aren't sent.
- The server simulates at 20 Hz and sends snapshots at 15 Hz; the client draws ships 110 ms in the past, interpolated between the two snapshots around that moment.

## Asteroid belts (owner)

- **Belt POIs:** 100 km across (`belts.js`). One per 12 players online, at least 3, placed anywhere inside the hexagon clear of its edge and at least 600 km from each other. They survive restarts (the `instances` table).
- **A half moon of rocks:** the rocks lie on a crescent around the POI's center, facing a random way: within 90° either side of its middle, at **45 km from the center, give or take 5 km** (owner: "near 50 km, give or take 5"; 45 keeps the crescent inside the POI's 50 km edge), thickest in the middle and thinning toward the horns.
- **Ore:** at least **2,000,000 m³** between all the rocks. **All five ores** are back: Cryonite, Pyroxite, Duranite, Hexite, Tantalite (keys ironstone, cuprite, cobaltine, iridite, starglass), each with its own rock art. A belt's main ore is picked by rarity (50 / 28 / 14 / 6 / 2), mixed with a couple of commoner ones.
- **Rock sizes follow their ore:** large (1000 m) from 20,000 m³, medium (500 m) from 5,000 m³, small (200 m) below that. A new large rock holds 25,000–60,000 m³, a medium 6,000–15,000, a small 1,500–4,000.
- **Wasting away (owner):** untouched for its first hour, then each rock loses ore steadily over 1–3 hours of its own, shrinking large → medium → small as it goes, until it's gone. Mining takes ore out of a rock too (it shrinks and goes the same way; wasting away counts what's been mined).
- **Mined out:** a belt with no rocks left **stops showing on the map** (and a replacement spawns), but **stays open while anyone's ships are inside**; it closes once they've left.
- **Who sees what (owner):** every belt still showing is on everyone's map as an asteroid-field marker (hover: its name). Its zone (a faint dashed circle) and rocks are only sent to, and drawn for, players with a ship inside it. Rocks are drawn at true size; when one would be under 6 px it's a dot, or its ore's icon when the belt is big on screen.

## The Library (owner)

A POI (`library.js`) that shows **every space object's art in rows**, always on everyone's map (map marker: the waypoint pin; hover: "Library"), 1,200 km north of the center, 60 km across. It never wastes away or closes, isn't saved (it's built at start-up), and doesn't count toward the belts.

- **The rows**, top to bottom, each centered, smallest to largest left to right: every ship hull in **blue** (Prospector, Wick, Bulwark, Rampart, Dredger, Excavator, Collier, Carrack, Beacon, Light Ark, Gleaner, Lodestar, Motherlode, Vigil, Heavy Ark, Capital Ark); the same in **red**; the **NPC law ships** (Deputy, Constable, Magistrate); the **structures and small objects** (Mining Drone ×2, Jettison Can off / on, Turret Base, Turret Head, Acceleration Gate, Stargate, Station blue / red); and the **asteroids** (each ore's rock at 200, 500 and 1000 m). Every sprite in `assets/ships` and `assets/rocks` is on a shelf. (Base-surface buildings and shipyard docks aren't space objects.)
- **True size:** the size each object had in the game before the reset, otherwise its art at 1.875 m per pixel (the scale nearly every sprite was drawn at). Rocks are their size wide, as in a belt.
- The shelves sit above the POI's center (clear of its marker); a warp drops out about 4 km below the bottom row.
- **Props:** drawn at true size, only sent to players with a ship inside (like a belt's rocks). Ships fly over them; a destination can't be on one. A single click picks one: gray brackets and "Name - distance". Nothing else: no menu, locking or Info.

## Align and warp (owner)

- **Align to a POI:** the ship turns and flies toward it at regular speed; the POI becomes its **destination**. It can warp there as long as the POI still advertises itself (shows on the map). If the POI stops showing, the destination goes and the ship **drifts on** at its speed and heading (no braking) until given a new order or stopped by the edge.
- **Warp to a POI:** align, and warp as soon as aligned: at least 75% of top speed, heading within 5° of the POI. You can't warp to a POI you're already in.
- **The warp:** a straight line, easing in and out, taking 2 s plus the distance at about 714 km/s (owner: crossing the system takes about 30 s). It drops out 30 km from the belt's center on the inside of its crescent (give or take a couple of km), at a dead stop, and the destination is cleared. No orders are taken mid-warp; ships in warp don't collide. A warping ship leaves a short fading streak.
- **Camera (owner):** following a ship through a warp zooms all the way out and holds the whole map still; when it drops out, the camera zooms back to where it was, onto the ship.
- **Where you order it:** right-click (or hold) a belt's (or the Library's) map marker → **Warp to name / Align to name**; or right-click (hold) space → **Belts ›**, a list of the belts: right-click (hold) one for its own menu, **Warp to / Align to** (owner: a list, not a third layer of submenus; a plain click on a name opens it too). That menu opens beside the name and the menus before it stay open until you pick something or move to another item in them. Under the Fleet window (below).

## Objects in space (owner)

Asteroids and ships (anyone's, including your other pilots' ships) are objects. **Right-click (or hold) one** for its menu, for the selected pilot's ship:

- **Lock Target:** shown while it's within targeting range (edge to edge) and the ship has a target free; the lock completes after the hull's lock time. Not mid-warp, not the ship itself, never the same object twice. Once locked, the menu shows **Unlock Target** instead; a lock in progress shows neither (owner: a lock in progress can't be canceled).
  - **Locks break** when the object leaves targeting range, is gone (a rock wasted away) or warps away. A ship that starts a warp drops all its locks.
  - Locked objects get **orange corner brackets** on the map (owner: orange = targeted), blinking while the lock comes on.
  - A ship you've locked is sent to you wherever it is, even outside your camera's view.
- **Align To (owner):** the ship **turns to face it and keeps the speed it had** (standing still, it only turns), tracking it if it moves; it slows to stop at the object's no-destination line rather than going over it.
- **Move towards:** fly straight to just off its edge (its no-destination line) and stop there; a ship keeps being followed.
- **Orbit** (owner): circle it at full speed, 0.5 km beyond its no-destination line, the way the ship was already turning; a moving object is followed.
  - All three replace any move or align order (and a move or POI order replaces them). They end if the object is gone.
- **Look At** (owner): the camera follows the object and zooms in and out about its center, until you pan (drag or WASD) or follow a ship. Library props have it as their only menu item; for a ship it's the same as following it.
- **Info:** a window titled with its name: an asteroid's ore icon, rarity, ore left (m³), size and belt (only for a belt you have a ship in); **a ship's hull** (owner): its icon, class, length and its general stats (as on the Fitting tab).
- **Pilot Info** (owner, another player's ship only): that ship's pilot in a "Pilot Info" window: portrait and name, Player (right-click / hold it for the player menu), Corp, Day Born, Total Endorsement Level (`GET /game/pilot-info/:id`).

## The HUD (owner)

Bottom center, for the selected pilot's ship:

- **Locked targets** on top, in the order they were locked (priority, left to right; **drag one to change its priority**, owner): the object's icon in a ring (blinking and filling while it locks, solid orange when locked) and its edge-to-edge distance underneath.
  - **×** unlocks a locked target; a target still locking has no × (owner).
  - Right-click (hold) a target for its menu: **Unlock Target**, Align To, Move towards, Orbit, Info.
  - Hovering it (touch: while the finger is down) shows its name and draws a **very thin, faint gray line from its ring on the HUD to the object** (owner).
- **Shield** and **Hull** bars with their numbers, and **Speed** (m/s, km/s from 10 km/s, which only a warp reaches). Hovering Speed shows **Max speed** (owner).
- **The hotbar:** the ship's **active** module slots (owner: modules are active or passive, and each hull has its own number of each; passive ones are only in the Fitting window), keyed **1, 2, 3…** along the number row (1–9, 0, -, =; not while typing); the Prospector has 4. **Empty slots are hidden unless a module is being dragged** (owner); with no modules in the game yet, it doesn't show. **While the Fitting window is open its empty slots and keys show too** (owner). **Click a fitted module (or press its key) to switch it on or off**; a running module is outlined orange with its cycle sweeping round. **Standby** (owner): a Mining Laser or Mining Drones switched on with no locked asteroid in range waits (a pulsing outline) and starts on the first one locked in range. A powered-off module is dimmed and can't be switched on. Drag modules on and off it like the Fitting window's slots.
- **Ship names (owner):** a ship is called "Pilot's Prospector" until it's renamed. **Rename** is in the right-click / hold menu of any of your ships, and of the ship's name on the Fitting window's title: a small "Rename Ship" window (Cancel / Rename). Names are 1–24 printable characters (whitespace squashed); an empty one puts the default back. Saved with the ship; only sent to you. Your ship menu (the selected pilot's own) is Look At, Show Fitting, Info, Rename.
- **Inventory** (owner: a window button in the left panel): a window titled with the ship's name for the selected pilot's ship: **a tab per hold** (owner: Ore Hold, Cargo Hold), that hold's fill bar (used / capacity) under the tabs, and a grid of its items (icon and amount; hover: the name) plus one empty cell. **Only the grid scrolls**; the window is always tall enough for at least one slot (owner). The last hold tab is remembered. **Drag an item within its grid to reorder it** (owner; the order is kept per hold and for the hangar, in this browser). Drag items onto module slots (from the cargo hold), into the Station, or between holds, **including onto another hold's tab** (owner); only a place that takes the item lights up while dragging (the ore hold takes only ore); right-click (hold) an item: **Fit** (a module in the cargo hold: the first free slot of its kind), **Move to ›** (the ship's other hold; docked, the Hangar and other docked ships' holds), **Info** (a module).
- **Fitting** (owner: a window button): a window titled with the selected pilot's ship's name, with four tabs (owner), the last one remembered. **The tabs always stay at the top.** On the Fitting tab (owner, desktop and phone alike) the left column holds the slots, the ship and the **Power** and **Disposition** fill bars (used / max), with the general stats **beside it on the right, scrolling on their own**; other tabs scroll below the tabs. The window can't be narrower than 6 module slots plus the stats column, or shorter than the left column down to the Disposition bar (owner). **Show Fitting** (owner) in the selected pilot's ship menu and in a fleet card's menu opens it on the Fitting tab (selecting that card's pilot).
  - **Fitting:** **active module slots above the ship's art** (keyed as on the hotbar), **passive slots below it**, each row's type (Active / Passive) small and gray inside its box (owner), the ship centered between them, close under the tabs, then its **general stats** (owner): Shields, Armor, Hull, Speed, Targeting Range, Targeting Time, Max Targets, **Drone Control Range** (owner; the Prospector: 20 km), **each hold** (owner). (Max power and disposition aren't here: they're the bars on the left and in the Stats tab.) **Refitting is done docked** (owner; at the Station or your own station): in space the fit shows but can't be changed ("Dock to refit"), only powered on / off, so slots can't be dragged and their menu is Power Off / On and Info. Docked: **drag a module from the cargo hold onto a slot to fit it**, from a slot onto another (moves it) or into the Inventory / Station to unfit it (it goes back in the cargo hold); right-click (hold) a fitted slot: **Unfit** (docked), **Power Off** / **Power On**, **Info**. **A powered-off module** (owner) keeps its disposition but uses no power and does nothing (dimmed on the slot and the hotbar); Power On is refused without the power for it. The Power and Disposition bars show what's fitted (with the pilot's licenses).
  - **Hull:** its name, class, description and **Hull Bonuses** per level of the class license (owner; the Prospector: +4% Mining Laser yield, +1% Mining Laser range, +10% Mining Drones yield per level, which at level 5 are its bonuses from before the reset; nothing uses them until modules return).
  - **Stats:** a **Fitting** section (owner): Active Slots, Passive Slots, Max Power, Max Disposition (no bars), then the ship's: then the ship's: Class, Length, Acceleration and the general stats.
  - **Requirements:** the licenses it needs (the Prospector: Mining Frigate I, as before the reset; the trainer's one license; the selected pilot has level 1 from the start, so it's checked) and the **Ark Ship** that carries it (owner: ships need an ark that can load them to fly them; the Prospector: Light Ark, a placeholder). **A green check** left of a requirement means the player has it (owner): the selected pilot's license at that level, or an ark of that type among your ships. Neither is enforced yet.

## Modules and mining (owner, 2026-10-11)

`modules.js`. Modules are items (5 m³ each) fitted into a hull's slots: **active** ones (Mining Laser, Mining Drones, Afterburner I) sit on the hotbar and are switched on and off; **passive** ones (Cargo Expander, Inertia Stabilizer, Mining Laser Upgrade, Mining Beam Extender, Mining Overclocker) always work. Each costs power and disposition from the hull's maximums. A fit past disposition is refused ("Not enough disposition"); one past power is fitted **powered off** ("Not enough power: fitted offline"). **Every ship gets a starter kit in its cargo hold, once** (owner): 2 Mining Lasers and one of every other module (45 m³ of its 50).

| Module | Slot | Power | Disp. | Does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mining Laser | active | 8 | 12 | 10 m³ every 15 s, 3 km |
| Mining Drones | active | 10 | 15 | 25 m³ every 60 s, 5 km |
| Afterburner I | active | 10 | 12 | +75% top speed while on |
| Cargo Expander | passive | 4 | 10 | +15% to both holds |
| Inertia Stabilizer | passive | 4 | 10 | +15% acceleration |
| Mining Laser Upgrade | passive | 5 | 10 | +5% laser yield |
| Mining Beam Extender | passive | 5 | 10 | +10% laser range |
| Mining Overclocker | passive | 6 | 10 | −8% laser cycle time |

- **Balance (owner: "balance it for me"):** untrained, two lasers + drones + afterburner (36 / 51) leave room for one Cargo Expander (exactly 40 power); with the module licenses at V a full 2 lasers + drones + afterburner + three upgrades fits (38.25 / 60.75). An untrained Prospector with 2 lasers and drones fills its ore hold in about 45 minutes.
- **Stacking:** two of the same passive count less (EVE's curve: 100%, 87%, 57%, 28%…).
- **Mining:** a Mining Laser or Mining Drones needs a **locked asteroid within its range** (the first such target) and room in the ore hold ("No locked asteroid in range" / "Ore hold full"). A laser's ore lands in the **ore hold** when its cycle completes (only what fits; a full hold stops it), and the rock loses that much (shrinking a size, gone when empty). A laser stops when the rock is gone, unlocked or out of range (it doesn't go back to standby). **Drones** (owner) need a locked rock within their 5 km to start, but once on it they **stay on it, locked or not, out to the hull's drone control range** (20 km), until it's gone, they're recalled or sent to another. **A warp switches every module off.** Everyone sees:
  - **a laser's beam** (owner), **blue**, from one of the Prospector's **four hardpoints** (two per arm; each laser slot uses an arm) to a point **on the rock's art itself** (never its transparent corners: `rockmasks.json`, made from the sprites by `scripts/gen/rockmasks.py`; re-run it if rock art changes) that it keeps until the next cycle (or until the rock shrinks a size), with a small square in the ore's color traveling along the beam from the rock to the ship once per cycle;
  - **a pair of drones** circling the rock along its no-destination line, facing it, each with a beam to the rock's center and a square of ore riding it once per cycle.
- **Drones haul their ore (owner):** when a drone cycle completes, the rock loses the yield and both drones fly it to the ship (carrying a square of ore), **circle the ship twice streaming the ore in** as little squares, and the ore lands in the ore hold when the streams are done (only what fits; a full hold stops them). Then they fly back to their place around the rock and only then start the next cycle. **They fly slower within 5 km of the ship** (0.75 km/s; 2 km/s farther out; the circling takes 4 s). A warp loses whatever they carry.
- **The drone key while they're out (owner):** if another locked asteroid is within drone range they **go to it** (flying over, then starting a cycle; mid-haul, they go there after dropping off); otherwise it **recalls** them: they fly home (dropping off anything they carry) and switch off once in.
- **Hull bonuses** (per Mining Frigate level): the Prospector's +4% laser yield, +1% laser range and +10% drone yield apply.
- **Licenses** (Licenses): Mining Lasers, Mining Drones, Mining Upgrades, Engineering Upgrades, Engines; none is needed to fit a module.
- **Info** on a module (its menu, or an item's): its slot, power and disposition (with the pilot's licenses), volume, what it does as fitted (a laser's yield, cycle and range with every bonus), and its license.
- Server notices ("Not enough power", "Ore hold full"…) show in the status line, not chat.

## The Station (owner, 2026-10-11, temporary)

`station.js`. A POI 1,200 km south of the center (map marker: the station icon) with the station at true size (2.77 × 1.65 km), solid for destinations like a Library prop. No market (owner: "just a singular hangar and ship list").

- **Dock:** right-click (hold) the station → Dock, or the **Dock button under the Fleet window** (owner), shown while the selected pilot's ship is within **5 km** of the station's edge. Out of range, Dock flies the ship there first and docks on arrival. A warp to the Station drops out 6 km south of it.
- **Docked:** the ship is out of space (others don't see it; no orders, locks or modules) until it **undocks** (the Station window's Undock button, or Undock in the ship's / fleet card's menu), a couple of km off the station's south side. Fitting still works.
- **The Station window** opens when the selected pilot's ship docks (or you come back docked) and closes when none of your ships is docked; right-click the station → Open Hangar reopens it. Left: **Hangar**, then the **Ship Bay** (owner): each ship docked there as a tab (click: show or hide its holds, expanded until you collapse it; remembered); right: the chosen one's items (a ship hold with its fill bar). **The list and the storage scroll on their own** (owner). **Right-click (hold) a ship's tab:** Show Fitting, Info, Rename, Undock, **Board Pilot / Unboard Pilot** (owner): a docked ship's pilot can leave it ("No pilot" on its tab; saved), and **a pilot who isn't aboard can't undock** (the Undock button is grayed; "Board the ship first"). The ship's own menu has Board / Unboard too while docked.
- **The hangar:** one per account, **unlimited** (owner); separate from your own station's hangar (Player stations). Move items between it and your docked ships' holds (and between a ship's own holds anywhere) by dragging onto a list entry or the storage, or right-click → Move to ›. **The ore hold takes only ore**; a ship's hold takes what fits and the rest stays. Saved with your ships.

## Celestials (owner, 2026-10-11)

`celestials.js`, laid out the same on every start from a fixed seed; nothing actually moves.

- **The sun** ("Atamus") at the Expanse's center, drawn again with the WebGL glow and lens flare from before the reset (`sun.js`, at the old 0.55 brightness; far off-screen only its glow reaches in from that side). **Nothing spawns within 1,000 km of it**: new ships now appear just south of the Station.
- **Five planets** (Atamus I–V; ocean, desert, ice or gas giant; radius 70–210 km) on orbit lines around the sun, **1–3 moons each** on orbit lines around their planet. Bodies are drawn at true size (never under a few pixels), lit from the sun; orbit lines are faint circles.
- **POIs and anchors** (owner): **the sun, each planet and each moon is a POI** at the body itself, on everyone's map (the body is its marker). Each has an anchor on a ring just off it (400 km off the sun, 140 km off a planet's surface, 50 km off a moon's): **aligning and warping to it head for the anchor**, not the body (a warp drops out there). The anchor's waypoint marker only shows **while one of your ships is inside that POI** (at the anchor; owner), and once it's clear of the body on screen.
- **Menus:** right-click (hold) the sun, a planet or a moon: Warp to / Align to and **Info** (type, world, radius, what it orbits and how far, its moons; the sun: star, radius, planets). The space menu has **Celestials ›** the sun (its Warp to / Align to / Info) and **Planets › a planet** (its own, and **Moons ›** each moon's).
- **Spawning:** asteroid belts now spawn **around planets and moons** (just beyond a planet's moons, or near a moon), never on a body or near the sun. Player stations keep out of each planet's area (the planet and its moons) and the sun's, but may sit along orbit lines or in deep space.

## Player stations (owner, 2026-10-11)

`home.js`. **Every account has its own station**: at each server start every account without one gets one (owner), and so does anyone new when they first play (none is made until the saved ones are loaded, so no one gets two). It starts **small**.

- **Where:** somewhere random in the Expanse, as far as can be found from every other player's station (at least 1,500 km while there's room; the farthest of a few hundred tries) and well clear of every other POI.
- **Its POI** is 50 km across, **on its owner's map only** (the station icon), so only they can align or warp to it (a warp drops out 4 km south of it). Anyone else can only find it by flying there; once a ship of theirs is inside its 50 km they see the station, but it isn't on their map and they can't dock.
- **The art** (`website/assets/stations/`, cut from the owner's reference sheets by `scripts/gen/stations.py` into `stations.json`): small, medium and large, each with its **arms retracted or extended** (the small one built from its full body on the parts sheet, docking port included, with the arm's tip cap or the whole arm in its socket), true size at 1.875 m per pixel (small: about 0.3 × 0.43 km), placed so the hub stays put when the arms move. The arms will later park ark ships; for now every station's are retracted.
- **Dock** (only at your own): right-click it → Dock, or the Dock button under the Fleet window within 5 km of its edge, as at the Station. Docked there: its own **hangar** (unlimited, kept with the station) and your ships docked there, in the station window titled with its name; **refit** there. Undocking leaves a couple of km off its south side. A ship docked at either station counts as a safe log-off, and is saved docked where it was.
- Stations are saved like belts (the `instances` table), with their storage.

## The game screen

- **Range rings (owner):** around the selected pilot's ship, its **targeting range** as a faint gray dash-dot circle, and one very faint dashed gray circle per kind of fitted active module that works on a target (Mining Laser, Mining Drones), at its range as fitted. Hovering that module on the hotbar or in the Fitting window (touch: pressing it) turns its circle orange and stronger. Ranges are edge to edge, so the rings sit that far out from the ship's edge.
- **Chat bubbles (owner):** a Local message shows in a bubble over the speaker's ship for anyone with a ship within 150 km of it, fading after a few seconds; one bubble per ship (the latest). With several of the speaker's (undocked) ships in one place (within 150 km of each other), **only one of them says it**: the selected pilot's, else the first; ships elsewhere each get one.
- **Smooth motion:** ships are drawn 150 ms behind the server, interpolated between snapshots; a late snapshot carries a ship on along its last motion briefly instead of freezing it. The estimate of the server's clock eases rather than jumps. Beams carry their cycle's (and a drone haul's) start time on the server's clock, so their squares and hauling drones move steadily.
- **Canvas:** the space backdrop, the hexagon's edge and the ships. Your ships are drawn in their blue sprite, others in red, at true scale; once a ship would be under 10 px long it's drawn as its hull icon instead. While one of your ships is flying, a faint dashed line runs to its destination, drawn even while the ship itself is off screen (owner: not while orbiting).

## Live updates (owner)

- Every push to `main` shows **every** player a 30 s "Update incoming" panel near the top of the screen. The number bounces each second.
- At zero, players are logged out to `play.html?updating=1`. It waits for the new build, then shows Enter System. If you're signed out by then (or the server doesn't answer for a minute), it goes to the login page instead. Enter System always loads a fresh (cache-busted) game page.
- Players who join mid-countdown see the time that's left.
- The server only starts a countdown if `main` is really ahead of what's live, once per commit. If it can't read GitHub, it counts down anyway.

## Logging off (owner)

- **Safe log-off:** every one of your ships docked. Your ships leave the world 20 s after you go (a reload within that keeps everything).
- **Otherwise** your ships stay in space for **5 minutes**, still doing what they were (flying, mining, holding locks), then each **safe-warps** about 1,500 km in a random direction (modules and locks off) and leaves the world when it lands. Coming back before then picks up where they are.

- **Who can do what:** any staff: see players and the log, kick, ban, unban, mute, freeze, Ban Name, set mutes / blocks, appeals, reports, support, request words. Admins (and the Head Admin) also: make / remove Moderators, suspend them, change licenses, credits and items, reset corporations, approve words. Only Nyssary: make / remove Admins. No one acts on themselves; staff don't act on staff.
- **Log tab** (`admin_log`): every kick, ban, unban, mute, freeze, appeal answer, role and stat change, newest first: when, the action, **the player (a link: click for their Info, right-click / hold for their menu)**, the admin and the reason. A ban whose player is still banned has an **Unban** button there (asks why; the unban is its own entry).
- **Appeals** (owner, `appeals.js`): **a conversation while the ban lasts.** A banned player trying to log in sees the ban (why, until when) and their appeal below it: their lines and every admin's, refreshing every 10 s, with a box to write (their login gives the page a 3-hour token). Every admin sees the whole conversation (the Appeals tab lists them, open first, with the last line; click one for its window) and can answer; **answering subscribes you** (or Subscribe / Unsubscribe): a subscribed admin gets a staff notification for each new line from the player (click it to open the appeal). **Accept** unbans; **Deny** closes it; either adds a last line with its reason. A closed appeal can't be written in: the player must start a new one. **A ban allows 3 appeals.** **Timeout**, on any appeal (owner): it closes and no new appeal can be made for a number of days, months or years; when that ends the player gets **one** more appeal. Unbanning someone accepts their open appeal. Answers and timeouts go to the log.

## Server hardening

- Malformed or hostile messages are ignored and logged, never fatal. Every command field is checked: coordinates must be finite numbers, the ship must be the sender's.
- Rejected promises are logged.
- Each connection is limited to about 40 commands a second, with a 16 KB message limit.
- Autosave runs every 10 s but skips the database write when nothing changed. Offline players get no snapshots.
- **Snapshots** leave out a ship's fit, holds and fitted stats, and the hangar, unless they changed since the last one sent to that session; the client keeps the last ones. Each account's ships are indexed once whenever ships come or go.
- Ship collisions are checked through a 1 km grid, so they stay cheap with many ships.
