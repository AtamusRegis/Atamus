# Atamus: how the game works today

This is a living spec of the game. Read it before every patch, and update it in the same commit as any change to behavior. Rules marked **(owner)** were decided explicitly by the owner, so keep them unless the owner says otherwise.

## The 3D rebuild (owner, 2026-10-12)

The owner wiped the 2D game to rebuild Atamus in 3D. **The whole 2D game — its world, client, UI, modules, combat, ETA, market, pilots, licenses and the rest — lives on the `legacy-2d` branch** (tip `e56e61d`); bring pieces back from there only when the owner asks. The accounts backend (auth, sessions, security, the admin API, the deploy countdown) was kept on `main` and still runs; the admin panel and chat have no frontend right now.


**Rebuilt 2026-10-15 (owner: "redo this all from scratch"):** the hexagon world, its background, stars and grid, movement, the camera and aiming were kept; everything else was cleared (on the `legacy-3d-v1` branch) and rebuilt around two teams whose ships are tuned in the Ship Editor.
**"Rebuild a bit" (owner, 2026-10-15, same day):** everyone flies the same ship, still on two teams; each player picks two weapons and a charged ability and buys upgrade levels with credits in the **Hangar**; asteroids and kills pay credits; capture points buff the team holding them; each team has a **station** (near it repairs, destroying the enemy's wins the match). The Ship Editor is gone: every number is fixed in `ships.js`.
**Old data is kept (owner):** accounts, credits, pilots, licenses and the old game tables stay in the database untouched for reference. The 3D game only reads and writes `users` (auth) and its own `world3d` table.

## The world (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **One shared 3D world on one server: no POIs, no systems, no instances.** Each player is sent only what is inside their **load circle** (below).
- **The border is the same flat-top hexagon** (flat edges top and bottom, points east and west), **2,500 m flat to flat for now** (owner, 2026-10-13; it was 126 km, and 5 miles before that), drawn as a glowing band on the ground. It stops players: movement walks as far along the step as still fits, and the sphere's edge rests on the line.
- **Each player is one ship** (owner, 2026-10-12: the owner's `Combat_Cruiser.glb`, Y up, +Z forward, in `website/models/`), **scaled to 100 m long**, resting on the grid and **painted its team's color** (every colored, not gray, material takes the team's hue: Blue blue, Yellow yellow). One per account; **it spawns near its team's station** (owner, 2026-10-15: entering the game, after being destroyed, switching team and at a new match; 170–280 m out on the side facing the middle, nose toward the middle). Positions are still saved every 10 s and on leaving, but no longer used (the Reset location checkbox on the Welcome back page has nothing left to do). Until the model loads, a blue sphere stands in.
- **Every ship is the same (owner, 2026-10-15):** 150 health, 1 health a second after 5 s without damage, 6 s respawn, 55 m/s (60 m/s² acceleration, 100 braking, 32°/s turning, 155°/s² turn acceleration, 60% strafe and reverse), 10% armor, 100 capacitor refilling 8 a second (`SHIP` in `ships.js`). A ship's numbers now are these with its **upgrade levels**, its **team's capture buffs** and its **active ability** laid over them; the server works them out every tick and sends each player its own (`{t:"stats"}`) to predict its movement with.
- **Movement (owner, 2026-10-13):** ships have momentum. Thrust accelerates at the ship's **Acceleration** toward the input's velocity; **a push off the nose gets only part of it** (full straight on, about half side-on, 30% against the nose). Top speed is **Max Speed** forward, **Strafe Speed** % of it sideways and **Reverse Speed** % backing up (blended between). Letting go of thrust brakes at **Braking**. While thrust is held the motion keeps its speed and swings toward the new direction at 0.875 rad/s; pushing more than 120° against the motion brakes it and builds up the other way. A / D turn the ship at up to **Turn Speed**, spinning up and easing off by **Turn Acceleration** (the server won't take a heading that turns faster). Hitting the border stops the ship dead.
- **The capacitor:** a bar at the bottom center (its number inside), **Max Capacitor** big, refilling **Capacitor Regen** a second, always. Firing the beam (4 a second) and the railgun (12 a shot) draws from it. The server keeps it (sent to its player 5 times a second); the client predicts it in between.
- **Colliders:** on the ground plane only (height ignored). Ships can't pass through Build shapes or each other: they stop or slide along (two ships that meet are each pushed half the overlap apart). A ship collides by an oval in its footprint; shapes by their outline. The server decides; the client predicts the same pushes for its own ship.
- **Travel is on the ground plane only (XY): no movement up or down on Z.**
- **Ship bumps (owner, 2026-10-14):** ships push each other: an impact trades momentum (equal masses, half a bounce), so a rammed ship is shoved away and slides to a stop. **Both ships take damage by how hard they closed on each other** (their velocities compared along the contact): (closing speed - 15 m/s) x 0.4, nothing below 15 m/s (scaled with the slower speeds, 2026-10-14). So catching up with a ship going the same way barely hurts (50 into 30: about 2 each), ramming a stopped one hurts more (50: about 16), and a head-on crash the most (50 each: about 34). The server decides; the client predicts the shove for its own ship. (`BUMP_E`, `BUMP_SAFE`, `BUMP_DMG` in world3d.js.) **Impacts throw sparks** (owner, 2026-10-14): where two ships meet (closing faster than 5 m/s), the server tells nearby players and their screens throw out white-gray debris and bright orange-yellow sparks that fly, arc down and burn out, more and faster the harder the hit; shown only if I can see one of the two ships (or it's mine). **Localized, in a cone (owner, 2026-10-14):** they start right where the hulls touched (the server sends that point, between each hull's point deepest toward the other) and spray in a cone (about ±30°) back toward the ship that drove in harder; railgun hits likewise spray back the way the slug came, from the exact impact point. **Shorter-lived, with black soot (owner, 2026-10-14):** sparks burn out in about 0.2–0.5 s and debris in 0.35–0.65 s; black soot particles (about a third as many again, drawn over the sparks) spray the same way and fade in 0.3–0.6 s.
- **Health (owner, 2026-10-14):** after 5 s without damage it regains its health regen a second. Damage the shield doesn't take is cut by armor % before it reaches the hull. **At 0 the ship is destroyed (owner, 2026-10-15: with an explosion):** a fireball with a bright core and a shockwave ring on the ground, sparks and soot thrown every way (a destroyed station's is much bigger); the ship disappears (it can't act, be hit or block anything) for 6 s, then comes back full at its team's station, materializing. Its drones go with it. **A health bar shows over any damaged ship** (mine too): green, then yellow under half, red under a quarter; a ship I can't see shows none, and the bar fades with the ship's visibility. **On a hit (owner, 2026-10-15)** the bar drops over 0.75 s (fast, easing out at the end), a white ghost of what was lost follows 0.4 s behind, the bar flashes white for 2 frames and is shoved about 9 px left before springing back; heals snap. **The ship itself flashes red on each hit, fading out over 4 frames.**
- **Player names (owner, 2026-10-15):** every other ship shows its player's name over it, in its team's color, above where its health bar goes; it fades with the ship's visibility, and a ship I can't see (or a destroyed one) shows none. My own ship has none.
- **Shields (owner, 2026-10-15):** now only from the **Shield ability** (below): 120 points that take damage before the hull (what's left of a hit carries on), for 6 s or until broken; no regeneration. While it's up, shots and beams stop on its **oval** (the ship's hitbox box grown by 1.42x plus 4 m, worked out by the server and sent to clients) instead of reaching the hull; a ship still materializing has none. It's **only seen when hit**: a flattened 3D oval around the ship, blue with a brighter hexagon pattern, **white where the hit landed**, spreading and fading back to its color, then fading out over 1.5 s (the far side shows through, dimmer). **Its color goes by how full it is: blue at half or more, yellow under half, red under a quarter.**
- **Space under the grid (owner, 2026-10-14):** the field is only its grid lines; under it is a deep blue nebula (from the owner's references) in parallax layers: furthest down a dark navy base with marbled blue clouds (swirled noise), dark dust lanes and large darker regions (owner: less saturated), then a layer of cyan glow spots and a nearer layer of faint blue wisps, each at its own depth so they slide past each other as you move; it drifts very slowly on its own. Between it and the grid is a field of very thin stars (white, blue, cyan, a few gold; a few bigger ones with a glow) at varied depths, so they parallax against the grid; each slowly dims in and out on its own. (Replaces the dark charcoal ground.)
- **Rendering for speed (owner, 2026-10-14):** the cloud noise is worked out once at start into a seamless 1024 × 1024 texture that the nebula only looks up; the three nebula layers are one plane (each pixel finds where its line of sight crosses each layer's depth); the grid lines are drawn in the same pass as the haze; nebula pixels behind the solid haze are skipped; the shadows are worked out again only when your ship has moved a meter or a block / Build piece changed; and the game draws at most 1.5 pixels per screen pixel. **Second pass (owner, 2026-10-14, aiming for 60 fps):** the colored space is drawn into a picture a sixth the screen's size each frame (owner, 2026-10-14: halved again from a third) and stretched behind everything (the stars stay sharp); clouds likewise into a third-size picture laid over the scene; the shadows are flat shapes drawn straight on the ground (no texture; the stencil buffer keeps overlaps from darkening); **the resolution adapts**: if frames come slower than about 50 a second over a couple of seconds the game draws fewer pixels (down to 60% of the screen's), and steps back up when there's room (a step up that's undone at once isn't retried for a minute); MSAA edge smoothing is used only on ordinary (not high-DPI) screens.
- **A respawned or materializing ship takes its visibility at once (owner, 2026-10-15):** a ship that jumps (more than 150 m between checks) starts from unseen, and while it materializes its visibility isn't faded, so its hologram (and shield) no longer show in a nebula or behind cover from where it was before.
- **Line of sight (owner, 2026-10-13):** on the ground plane (height ignored, like the colliders). Build shapes (and nebula cores) cast shadows from their outlines, from their far side only (the object and its footprint are never shaded); ships cast none. **Mutual visibility (owner, 2026-10-14):** if a player can see you, you can see them, and the less of each other you can see, the more see-through you are to each other. The pair's visibility, the same both ways: **the line between the two pivots counts for 20%** (owner, 2026-10-14; was half); for the other 80%, each ship's **visibility points** (the saved ones from the old Object Editor, else its box's corners and side middles) send a line to the other ship's pivot, and the share of those not cut by a Build shape counts. Each other ship is drawn whole, its opacity **rising smoothly from 0 at 25% visible to solid at 75%** (owner, 2026-10-14; no steps), **fading in fast (about 0.15 s) and out slowly (about 0.6 s)** so it shows promptly but doesn't flicker at corners. The minimap arrows follow the same opacity. Worked out 30 times a second. Client-side only for now: the server still sends every ship in the load circle. **A ship I can't see (hidden by visibility) doesn't stop my aim line** (owner, 2026-10-14), so the aim line can't be used to find ships before they show.
- **Unseen ground is shaded (owner, 2026-10-13):** the shadow areas get a flat 50% black (owner, 2026-10-14: was a drifting dusty blue / purple texture, then a slightly blurred see-through black from a shadow texture), and everything past the map's edge a flat see-through black layer over the grid. Where shadows overlap they don't get darker. The map's border band is drawn over it (owner, 2026-10-14), so the edge always shows.
- **Sight fades with distance (owner, 2026-10-14):** starting 200 m past the leash circle (900 m from my ship) the haze ramps up from clear to fully solid 400 m further out (1,300 m). Nothing past 1,300 m is drawn (other ships, test ships, blocks, Build pieces), and the minimap leaves out ships past it too.
- The grid is drawn by the ground's own shader (anti-aliased 100 m lines on the surface itself), after a separate layer of rendered lines flickered against the ground.
- **Server-authoritative:** the client only sends a direction and its ship's heading (`{t:"move", x, y, b, h}`, direction clamped to length 1; the heading may turn no faster than the team's Turn Speed allows); **after movement or rotation stops, the settled state is sent twice more** (owner, 2026-10-13) so the server and everyone else land on the real final position and heading. Other screens draw each ship with the heading its owner sent (snapshot rows: `[id, x, y, heading, health, shield, beam, materializing, dead, max health, active ability, chained ship]`; an invisible enemy isn't sent at all); the server simulates at 20 Hz and sends each client its own snapshot at 15 Hz (only the ships in its load circle). **Less traffic (owner, 2026-10-15):** a snapshot carries only what changed since that client's last one (the ships whose row changed; the ids of the ones exactly as before; or just "same" when nothing moved), and the client rebuilds the full list; each player's own capacitor / ammo / reload message goes out only when it changes; impacts go as one message a tick; a changed object is written once and sent to everyone who needs it. Big messages (the hello, lists) are compressed. The client predicts its own sphere locally (with the same border rule) and is softly corrected by snapshots; other ships keep a short history of snapshot positions timed by the server's clock and are drawn 150 ms in the past, interpolated between the two snapshots around that moment (briefly carried on along their last motion if a snapshot is late); they turn only on frames where they really moved.
- **Smooth but responsive (owner, 2026-10-14):** the nose turns on a spring (spins up to its 0.5 rad/s within about a fifth of a second and eases into the target, never overshooting; owner: a little less snappy) instead of starting and stopping dead; **ships lean into their turns** (the model rolls with the turn rate, up to about 18°, smoothed); the server's small corrections to my own ship are worked off smoothly over about 0.1 s every frame instead of in jumps at each snapshot; other ships follow a smooth curve through the snapshots (Catmull-Rom) instead of straight lines between them. Physics (speeds, acceleration, braking) is unchanged.
- A player leaving (socket closed) despawns at once; their position is saved.
- **The load circle (owner, 2026-10-13; 1,300 m):** the server sends each player only the ships and Build pieces inside it; everything beyond isn't sent at all. **It ends where the distance haze turns solid** (the 700 m leash circle + 200 m + 400 m), so anything inside it is already loaded while still hidden in the haze. Pieces come and go as ships move (checked twice a second). (Its ring shows on the minimap only.) A ship that enters is placed where it is; one that leaves is hidden. Chat, joins and leaves still reach everyone.

## Sides and the minimap (owner, 2026-10-13)

- **Teams: Blue or Yellow, remembered per account** (owner, 2026-10-13; Red became Yellow on 2026-10-15: its saved key stays "red", like `exumers`). The first time, a player picks one ("Choose a side", two buttons over the world); after that **the game re-joins the remembered team by itself**. **A "Reset faction" checkbox under Enter System on the Welcome back page** brings the picker back for that entry (`game.html?reset=1`). The team is saved in `world3d.team`. **Switching in the game (owner, 2026-10-15, "for now"):** two buttons at the top left, **Blue** and **Yellow** (yours lit), switch your team (at most every 2 s): your ship is full again and materializes at the new team's station, and everyone repaints it.
- **The minimap:** the map's hexagon at the bottom left, **always north up**. It shows **your load circle** and **every ship inside it as an arrow pointing the way it faces, in its team's color**; yours with a white outline, plus a short white tick for where the camera faces. **Also (owner, 2026-10-15):** each team's station (a square in its color), every asteroid (gray dots) and the capture points (circles filled with the holding team's color). Redrawn ten times a second. **Scrolling over the minimap zooms it** (owner, 2026-10-13), not the game: from the whole map in until your load circle just fits inside, **your ship always at its center unless fully zoomed out**; kept per browser. **The minimap keeps its hexagon shape at every zoom** (zoomed in, the map's own border shows as a gray hexagon inside the blue frame where it's in view).
- Ships themselves look the same for both sides for now.
- PTR only: `game.html?as=Name` connects as another account (for testing several players locally).

## Controls (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **Ship-relative controls (owner, 2026-10-14): W thrusts the way the ship faces, S flies it backward, A and D turn it**; **Q and E push it to its left and right without turning it** (owner, 2026-10-14: strafing; the sideways top speed and a part of the thrust, like any push off the nose; with W or S the pushes add up, never faster than one; Q stays the Build menu's variation key while that's open); **backing up, A and D swap** (owner, 2026-10-14: like a car in reverse, backing up with A swings the ship back toward its left) (left / right at 0.5 rad/s, owner 2026-10-14: cut by 75% twice; a full turn takes about 12.5 s; spinning up and easing off over about 0.2 s). Backing up runs at the side / back rate (60%) with the reduced thrust for pushing against the nose, and can't sprint. (Was: WASD moved the ship relative to the camera, with the nose turning to face the push; the reversal-through-the-middle rule went with it.)
- **Combat keys (owner, 2026-10-15):** **left click** (held) fires the primary weapon, **F** (held) the secondary, **Shift** uses the ability, **R** reloads both weapons, **H** (or the **Hangar** button under the team buttons) opens the Hangar. Shift no longer boosts.
- **The middle mouse button, dragged, rotates the camera around the player** — **inverted** (owner, 2026-10-12): dragging right turns the view right. On touch, a one-finger drag does the same (mobile parity: drag = drag). *Open: how movement itself works on touch — nothing is decided yet.*
- **The camera orbits the player looking down toward it** at pitch 60°, rotation 60% (owner: "60d: 600-1800m: 60%"), **600 m out at most; the mouse wheel (touch: pinch) zooms in to 200 m** (owner, 2026-10-13; it was 600–1,800 m, then fixed at 600). **Aiming keeps the wheel zoom** while the aim point is inside the 300 m dead zone (owner, 2026-10-14; it used to ease back to 600 m on every aim); past it the camera reaches out (below), and releasing returns to the wheel zoom. The wheel does nothing while aiming. Aiming still pulls the camera out as described below. No settings panel (owner, 2026-10-13: removed with the weapons); the leash and overflow use fixed values.
- **Right-click aiming (owner, 2026-10-13: weapons and the rest of aiming scrapped, the leash camera brought back):** holding the right button draws a **dashed white line from the ship's center on the ground plane** (owner: not the model's center) **to the reticle, fading out over its last 40%** (owner, 2026-10-13: no dot at the end any more; the dashes scale with zoom). **If a block or another ship is in the way (owner, 2026-10-13), the line is white up to where it would hit and red from there to the reticle** (no fade). **The reticle is the ground point under the pointer, clamped to the blue camera leash circle** (700 m around the ship; shown only with Weapon helpers on): push past it and the reticle rides its edge. The ship doesn't turn toward it (owner, 2026-10-14: the reticle is independent of the ship).
  - **The camera moves toward the reticle** (as before the scrap): its reach is solved from the pointer's screen direction and constants. **A 300 m dead zone around the ship (owner, 2026-10-14; was 30% of the screen):** while the aim point is within 300 m of the ship the camera stays put; past it the camera reaches out, to the full reach by 150 m further, and back in as the aim point comes inside again. **Toward the screen's edge it always reaches out fully** (from 70% to 95% of the way to the edge), since in some directions (toward the bottom, or zoomed in) the screen ends before 300 m (owner, 2026-10-14). The distance is measured as if the camera were at rest on the ship at the wheel zoom, so the camera reaching out never changes it (owner, 2026-10-14: measuring from the moving camera made it jump between in and out). **Reaching out slides the camera along the aim (the leash) but never zooms it** (owner, 2026-10-14; it used to zoom out up to 6,000 m too): the focus slides toward the reticle until the screen's edge ahead sits the overflow past the distance circle, at any zoom (owner, 2026-10-14): zoomed in it slides further and the ship may leave the screen, so the reticle can always reach the circle. Releasing eases everything back.
  - **The camera trail** (owner, 2026-10-13): while the ship moves and the aim isn't steering the camera (not aiming, or the pointer inside the dead zone), the camera trails **behind the ship along its motion, 0–80 m with speed** (owner, 2026-10-13: was up to 200; 0.4 m per m/s, so 80 m at full speed, capped there when sprinting), like a camera lagging behind (the ship sits ahead of the screen center). It fades out as the aim takes over past the dead zone, and eases back when the ship stops. Not while the middle mouse holds the frame.
  - The reticle is re-projected after the camera takes its final pose each frame, so it never trails the pointer.
  - **Middle mouse while aiming:** the camera frame (zoom and the focus's offset) freezes and orbits the player with the yaw; the cursor is pointer-locked at the press spot and the reticle stays raycast from that screen spot; lifting the middle button frees the cursor.
- **Materializing after a respawn (owner, 2026-10-14):** a ship that respawns (destroyed, or an admin kill / respawn; not joining) appears as a cyan hologram revealed **from its nose to its tail** (the first 60% of 3 s, a bright line at the reveal's edge, drifting scan bands), then **solidifies** into the real ship over the rest. Until it's done it can't move, turn or fire, and it takes no damage. The server keeps the timer (sent with the snapshots as the time left); everyone near sees it.
- **Weapon helpers** (owner, 2026-10-13: "make a toggle for weapon ui helpers"): a checkbox at the top right. **Off by default**; for now it shows or hides the blue leash circle. Kept per browser (`atamus.helpers`); hidden on touch.
- **The cursor** (owner, 2026-10-13): the system cursor is always hidden on the game page; **a small white dot** (4 px, faint dark outline) sits wherever the pointer is, over the UI too (buttons, the chat, the side picker). It stays put while the middle mouse holds the pointer lock and disappears when the pointer leaves the window. Not on touch.
- **The browser's own right-click menu never opens on the game page**, over the UI either (owner, 2026-10-13); only the game's own menus (a name in chat) open.
- **Touch devices (owner, 2026-10-13: "this will be a PC game, just using mobile to test as a second account"):** a **joystick** at the bottom left drives movement like WASD (analog, small dead zone; forward = where the camera faces); **dragging anywhere else turns the camera**, two fingers on the world pinch the zoom. The minimap moves to the top left. **Right-click has no touch version, by the owner's decision** (an exception to mobile parity), and there's no sprint on touch. **A tablet with a mouse is played like a PC (owner, 2026-10-15):** touch mode (the joystick, the touch layout, staff windows hidden) only applies when the device has no mouse at all, and the first mouse movement switches it off for the session.

## Weapons (owner, 2026-10-15: "rebuild a bit")

- **Each player picks two of five weapons in the Hangar: a primary (left click) and a secondary (F)**, both held to fire; they can't be the same. **Each has its own magazine and reload** (an empty one reloads by itself; **R** reloads both early). Changing a weapon applies at once and the new one starts reloading. Numbers in `WEAPONS` (`ships.js`); damage and range go up with upgrades and buffs.
- **Bullets:** 9 damage, 6 shots a second, 40 rounds, 2 s reload; full damage to 450 m, dropping to 40% by 650 m; 1,300 m/s slowing to 10% past optimal; spread from 0 up to 8° each way (0.8° a shot, closing 6°/s; the cone is drawn around the aim line while aiming). Short tracers; sparks where they hit, a white spot on a shield, a puff on a wall.
- **Beam:** held on, 40 damage a second to 600 m, dropping to 35% by 850 m; drains 12 ammo a second from 100 (3 s reload) and 4 capacitor a second; **turns toward the aim at 110°/s** (the server turns it; a new beam starts where you aim). A white core in a glow of the team's color, thinning toward its end.
- **Railgun:** hits at once along its whole reach: 65 damage to 1,000 m, dropping to 60% by 1,250 m; one shot every 1.25 s, 4 per magazine, 3 s reload, 12 capacitor a shot. A bright line that fades in about 0.4 s.
- **Missiles:** fired one at a time (1.2 a second, 4 per magazine, 4 s reload); each **zigzags out at 260 m/s, homing on the enemy nearest its nose** (ships and the enemy station within 700 m, inside 70°), and **bursts** on whatever it touches, on reaching its target or after 4 s: 45 damage at the middle, half at the 70 m edge, to enemy ships (a shield takes it first; walls shelter), asteroids and the enemy station. 15 health: shots can bring one down.
- **Drones:** each shot launches one (2 a second, 3 per magazine, 6 s reload; **at most 3 out at once**). A drone **flies to the enemy nearest the aim** (else the nearest one), **circles it zigzagging and shoots it** (7 damage every 0.5 s within 170 m, a flickering line), and **wears out** (25 health, losing 2.5 a second; shots bring it down sooner). With no enemy near it circles its ship. It doesn't chase far, and goes when its ship dies, switches team or unfits drones.
- **What stops a shot:** Build shapes, asteroids, the enemy station, enemy missiles and drones, and enemy ships (their shield oval while it's up, else their hitbox; **point-blank, from inside the hitbox, still hits**). **Shots pass through teammates and your own station: no friendly fire** (ship bumps still hurt both). **A ship the shooter can't see** (mutual visibility, blocked by Build shapes) **isn't hit**: bullets, beams and railgun shots pass through it (missiles and drones don't care). Not while the Build menu or Hangar is open, typing, materializing or dead. The client sends only directions; the server checks everything as hostile.
- **While aiming, the aim line runs out to the primary weapon's reach** (white, fading at the end; red past whatever it would hit first). It turns toward the pointer at 540°/s.
- **The bars under the capacitor (owner, 2026-10-15: a reload bar):** one per weapon, with its name and the rounds left (the beam: %), filling back up in amber while it reloads; and the ability's: its name, filling as it charges (gray), green when ready, draining white-green while it runs.

## Abilities (owner, 2026-10-15)

- **Each player picks one in the Hangar; Shift uses it.** It **charges up** (the bar fills) and can only be used full; using it empties it, and it charges again once it's over. Changing ability starts the new one empty. Numbers in `ABILITIES` (`ships.js`).
- **Shield** (18 s charge): a 120-point shield for 6 s (the oval, as above), gone when broken.
- **Damage Reduction** (18 s): half of the damage still left after armor is cut too, for 5 s.
- **Speed Boost** (12 s): 60% more speed and acceleration (and some turning) for 4 s.
- **Damage Boost** (20 s): 40% more damage for 5 s.
- **Teleport** (10 s): jumps straight ahead along the nose, up to 250 m (shorter if that spot is blocked or off the map; not used if nowhere fits), keeping its motion; a cyan flash where it left and where it lands.
- **Invisibility** (22 s): for 6 s the server stops sending the ship to enemies (it's gone from their screens and minimap; missiles and drones ignore it); teammates and its pilot see it faint. **Firing ends it.**
- **Chain** (18 s): chains the enemy ship nearest the aim (within 450 m and 35°, one I can see; not used if there's none) for 4 s: a glowing line between them, and **the chained ship is pulled in at 90 m/s whenever it's more than 110 m away** (its pilot's screen predicts the pull). It breaks if either dies or they get 675 m apart.

## Credits, upgrades and the Hangar (owner, 2026-10-15)

- **Credits:** **destroying an asteroid** pays 0.8 per meter of its radius (about 14–34); **destroying an enemy ship** pays 100. Kept per account (`world3d.credits`; the 2D game's credits stay untouched), shown by the capacitor bar (gold) and in the Hangar.
- **Upgrades, levels 1–10:** Health (+10% a level), Speed (+4%), Armor (+4 points), Damage (+6%), Health Regen (+1.5 a second), Energy Regen (+12% capacitor regen). Level L to L + 1 costs 40 x L credits (40 … 360). Applied at once (more health comes in full).

Right-click on ships stays aiming.
