# Atamus: how the game works today

This is a living spec of the game. Read it before every patch, and update it in the same commit as any change to behavior. Rules marked **(owner)** were decided explicitly by the owner, so keep them unless the owner says otherwise.

## The 3D rebuild (owner, 2026-10-12)

The owner wiped the 2D game to rebuild Atamus in 3D. **The whole 2D game — its world, client, UI, modules, combat, ETA, market, pilots, licenses and the rest — lives on the `legacy-2d` branch** (tip `e56e61d`); bring pieces back from there only when the owner asks. The accounts backend (auth, sessions, security, the admin API, the deploy countdown) was kept on `main` and still runs; the admin panel and chat have no frontend right now.

**Old data is kept (owner):** accounts, credits, pilots, licenses and the old game tables stay in the database untouched for reference. The 3D game only reads and writes `users` (auth) and its own `world3d` table.

## The world (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **One shared 3D world on one server: no POIs, no systems, no instances.** Each player is sent only what is inside their **load circle** (below).
- A flat ground plane with a **2D grid** (100 m cells) drawn on it. **Colors (owner, 2026-10-13, from a reference image):** a dark charcoal ground and background (#18181e) with soft gray lines (about #3f3f45 at their brightest); the far ground fades into the same charcoal. The grid shader outputs in sRGB, so the colors show as specified.
- **The border is the same flat-top hexagon** (flat edges top and bottom, points east and west), **2,500 m flat to flat for now** (owner, 2026-10-13; it was 126 km, and 5 miles before that), drawn as a glowing band on the ground. It stops players: movement walks as far along the step as still fits, and the sphere's edge rests on the line.
- **Each player is one ship** (owner, 2026-10-12: the owner's `Combat_Cruiser.glb`, Y up, +Z forward, in `website/models/`), **scaled to 100 m long**, resting on the grid and **turning to face the way it travels at a fast fixed rate** (owner: "almost instantly, but not instantly"; **0.5 rad/s**, owner: first 12, then 4, 8, 4, 2, and 0.5 on 2026-10-14; a U-turn takes about 6.3 s), pivoting about the hull's middle. The model's nose is -Z, flipped on load. **A and D turn the ship (owner, 2026-10-14); the reticle is independent of it.** The heading also sets how much thrust a push gets (see Weight), and others see it through the synced heading. **With neither aiming nor WASD, it keeps the last direction it faced** (owner, 2026-10-12): only held input re-aims your ship, so network-correction nudges never turn it; other ships re-aim only at a sustained real speed. Everyone flies the same ship for now. One per account; it spawns within 300 m of the center the first time and comes back where it was after that — unless **Reset location** is ticked under Enter System on the Welcome back page (owner, 2026-10-13), which puts it back within 300 m of the center for that entry (`game.html?resetpos=1`, sent with the side; dropped from the address once used, so reconnects don't reset again) (positions saved every 10 s and on leaving; a saved position outside the hexagon respawns at the center). Until the model loads, a blue sphere stands in. The server still treats players as 50 m circles for the border.
- **Travel is on the ground plane only (XY): no movement up or down on Z.**
- **Speed: 50 m/s forward** (owner, 2026-10-14: all movement cut to 25% to slow the game down; was 300, then 200; `SPEED` in `world3d.js`). Crossing the map takes about 50 seconds at full speed. **Forward is where the ship faces** (owner, 2026-10-14): full speed thrusting with W; backing up with S runs at 60% (`SIDE_SPEED`). The client sends the nose's direction as "forward" with each move.
- **Weight (owner, 2026-10-13; all of it cut to 25% on 2026-10-14):** ships have momentum. Thrust accelerates at **50 m/s²** toward the input's velocity (0 to full speed in about 1 s straight), but **a push off the nose gets only part of it**: full when the nose points where it's pushed, about half side-on, **30% pushing against the nose**. **Letting go of WASD brakes at least 100 m/s²**, however the nose points, and **a stop always ends within 25 m, whatever the speed**: the braking rate is fixed when WASD is let go at the harder of 100 m/s² and the rate that stops the current speed in 25 m (50 m/s stops in 12.5 m / 0.5 s, a 65 m/s sprint in about 21 m). Pressing WASD again ends the stop. (`STOP_D` in world3d.js, sent in the hello.) Online, the ship also covers the distance flown before the server hears the release (half a round trip). The nose turns toward the **input** direction, not the drift. **Changing direction keeps the momentum** (owner, 2026-10-13): while WASD is held, the ship's motion keeps its speed and swings toward the new direction at **0.875 rad/s** (owner, 2026-10-14: was 8, then 3.5 for a slight drift, then cut to 25%; a 90° change of motion takes about 1.8 s), **pushing more than 120° against the motion (S while flying forward) brakes it and then builds up the other way** (owner, 2026-10-14; it used to swing round in a long U-turn, so S kept the ship going forward). Its speed meanwhile eases toward the target speed for that direction: up at the thrust above (scaled by how far the push is off the nose), down at the braking rate. From rest it sets off straight along the input. (`carry` / `VEL_TURN` in world3d.js, mirrored in the client.) Server and client integrate the same way (`ACCEL`, `BRAKE`, `TURN_ACCEL` in world3d.js, sent in the hello); hitting the border still stops the ship dead.
- **The capacitor (owner, 2026-10-13):** a 0–100 bar at the bottom center. **Sprinting drains it 1 for every 100 m covered** (owner, 2026-10-13; was 20 a second: a full bar is 10 km of sprint, about 38 s at 260 m/s), **moving without sprinting recharges it slowly** (4 a second), **sitting still faster** (12 a second). Run dry and sprint stays off until it's back to 15. The server enforces it (each player is sent their own charge 4 times a second, to them only) and the client predicts it the same way.
- **Sprint (owner, 2026-10-12): holding Shift makes the ship 30% faster** (owner, 2026-10-13; now 65 m/s, was double; `SPRINT` in world3d.js), **forward only** (owner, 2026-10-13: within 45° of forward — any direction when not aiming, toward the reticle when aiming; otherwise Shift does nothing and the capacitor isn't drained) — **but not while aiming** (owner, 2026-10-13: the right button held turns sprint off; the client sends no sprint flag then). The sprint is just a flag to the server (`{t:"move", x, y, b}`); the server applies the speed-up. *Open: its touch equivalent.* (Since 2026-10-14 forward is the nose: sprint works thrusting forward with W, not backing up; it works while aiming too.)
- **X drops a block** (owner, 2026-10-12: a movement reference "for now"; shared, 2026-10-13): a 50 m block at the player's position, kept by the server and seen by everyone (30 per player, the oldest goes; 400 in all; gone when the server restarts). The ship that dropped it is pushed out of it. **For staff, X opens the Object Editor instead** (owner, 2026-10-14).
- **Colliders (owner, 2026-10-13):** on the ground plane only (height ignored). Ships can't pass through blocks (50 m squares), Build pieces or each other: they stop or slide along (two ships that meet are each pushed half the overlap apart). Each ship collides by the ship's Movement collider (Object Editor: an oval, box or capsule; it starts as a 45 m circle), pieces by their kind's Movement collider. The server decides; the client predicts the same pushes for its own ship.
- **Ship bumps (owner, 2026-10-14):** ships push each other: an impact trades momentum (equal masses, half a bounce), so a rammed ship is shoved away and slides to a stop. **Both ships take damage by how hard they closed on each other** (their velocities compared along the contact): (closing speed - 15 m/s) x 0.4, nothing below 15 m/s (scaled with the slower speeds, 2026-10-14). So catching up with a ship going the same way barely hurts (50 into 30: about 2 each), ramming a stopped one hurts more (50: about 16), and a head-on crash the most (50 each: about 34). The server decides; the client predicts the shove for its own ship. (`BUMP_E`, `BUMP_SAFE`, `BUMP_DMG` in world3d.js.) **Impacts throw sparks** (owner, 2026-10-14): where two ships meet (closing faster than 5 m/s), the server tells nearby players and their screens throw out white-gray debris and bright orange-yellow sparks that fly, arc down and burn out, more and faster the harder the hit; shown only if I can see one of the two ships (or it's mine). **Localized, in a cone (owner, 2026-10-14):** they start right where the hulls touched (the server sends that point, between each hull's point deepest toward the other) and spray in a cone (about ±30°) back toward the ship that drove in harder; railgun hits likewise spray back the way the slug came, from the exact impact point. **Shorter-lived, with black soot (owner, 2026-10-14):** sparks burn out in about 0.2–0.5 s and debris in 0.35–0.65 s; black soot particles (about a third as many again, drawn over the sparks) spray the same way and fade in 0.3–0.6 s.
- **Health (owner, 2026-10-14):** 100 per ship (`HP_MAX`), sent with each ship in the snapshots. At 0 the ship is back at full health near the center. No regeneration yet. **A health bar shows over any damaged ship** (mine too): green, then yellow under half, red under a quarter; a ship I can't see shows none, and the bar fades with the ship's visibility.
- **Space under the grid (owner, 2026-10-14):** the field is only its grid lines; under it is a deep blue nebula (from the owner's references) in parallax layers: furthest down a dark navy base with marbled blue clouds (swirled noise), dark dust lanes and large darker regions (owner: less saturated), then a layer of cyan glow spots and a nearer layer of faint blue wisps, each at its own depth so they slide past each other as you move; it drifts very slowly on its own. Between it and the grid is a field of very thin stars (white, blue, cyan, a few gold; a few bigger ones with a glow) at varied depths, so they parallax against the grid; each slowly dims in and out on its own. (Replaces the dark charcoal ground.)
- **Rendering for speed (owner, 2026-10-14):** the cloud noise is worked out once at start into a seamless 1024 × 1024 texture that the nebula only looks up; the three nebula layers are one plane (each pixel finds where its line of sight crosses each layer's depth); the grid lines are drawn in the same pass as the haze; nebula pixels behind the solid haze are skipped; the shadows are worked out again only when your ship has moved a meter or a block / Build piece changed; and the game draws at most 1.5 pixels per screen pixel. **Second pass (owner, 2026-10-14, aiming for 60 fps):** the colored space is drawn into a picture a sixth the screen's size each frame (owner, 2026-10-14: halved again from a third) and stretched behind everything (the stars stay sharp); clouds likewise into a third-size picture laid over the scene; the shadows are flat shapes drawn straight on the ground (no texture; the stencil buffer keeps overlaps from darkening); **the resolution adapts**: if frames come slower than about 50 a second over a couple of seconds the game draws fewer pixels (down to 60% of the screen's), and steps back up when there's room (a step up that's undone at once isn't retried for a minute); MSAA edge smoothing is used only on ordinary (not high-DPI) screens.
- **Line of sight (owner, 2026-10-13):** on the ground plane (height ignored, like the colliders). Blocks and Build pieces cast shadows from their Sight outlines, from their far side only (the object and its footprint are never shaded); ships cast none. **Mutual visibility (owner, 2026-10-14):** if a player can see you, you can see them, and the less of each other you can see, the more see-through you are to each other. The pair's visibility, the same both ways: **the line between the two pivots counts for 20%** (owner, 2026-10-14; was half); for the other 80%, each ship's **Visibility points** (Object Editor) send a line to the other ship's pivot, and the share of those not cut by a block or Build piece counts. Each other ship (and test ship) is drawn whole, its opacity **rising smoothly from 0 at 25% visible to solid at 75%** (owner, 2026-10-14; no steps), **fading in fast (about 0.15 s) and out slowly (about 0.6 s)** so it shows promptly but doesn't flicker at corners. The minimap arrows follow the same opacity. Worked out 30 times a second. **With the Object Editor open** each other ship's lines to my pivot are drawn, plus the pivot-to-pivot line (mine to them aren't drawn, owner 2026-10-14; clear green, blocked red) with the percentage over each ship, for testing. Client-side only for now: the server still sends every ship in the load circle. **A ship I can't see (hidden by visibility) doesn't stop my aim line** (owner, 2026-10-14), so the aim line can't be used to find ships before they show.
- **Unseen ground is shaded (owner, 2026-10-13):** the shadow areas get a flat 50% black (owner, 2026-10-14: was a drifting dusty blue / purple texture, then a slightly blurred see-through black from a shadow texture), and everything past the map's edge a flat see-through black layer over the grid. Where shadows overlap they don't get darker. The map's border band is drawn over it (owner, 2026-10-14), so the edge always shows.
- **Sight fades with distance (owner, 2026-10-14):** starting 200 m past the leash circle (900 m from my ship) the haze ramps up from clear to fully solid 400 m further out (1,300 m). Nothing past 1,300 m is drawn (other ships, test ships, blocks, Build pieces), and the minimap leaves out ships past it too.
- The grid is drawn by the ground's own shader (anti-aliased 100 m lines on the surface itself), after a separate layer of rendered lines flickered against the ground.
- **Server-authoritative:** the client only sends a direction and its ship's heading (`{t:"move", x, y, b, h}`, direction clamped to length 1, heading wrapped); **after movement or rotation stops, the settled state is sent twice more** (owner, 2026-10-13) so the server and everyone else land on the real final position and heading. Other screens draw each ship with the heading its owner sent (snapshots carry `[id, x, y, heading]`); the server simulates at 20 Hz and sends each client its own snapshot at 15 Hz (only the ships in its load circle). The client predicts its own sphere locally (with the same border rule) and is softly corrected by snapshots; other ships keep a short history of snapshot positions timed by the server's clock and are drawn 150 ms in the past, interpolated between the two snapshots around that moment (briefly carried on along their last motion if a snapshot is late); they turn only on frames where they really moved.
- **Smooth but responsive (owner, 2026-10-14):** the nose turns on a spring (spins up to its 0.5 rad/s within about a fifth of a second and eases into the target, never overshooting; owner: a little less snappy) instead of starting and stopping dead; **ships lean into their turns** (the model rolls with the turn rate, up to about 18°, smoothed); the server's small corrections to my own ship are worked off smoothly over about 0.1 s every frame instead of in jumps at each snapshot; other ships follow a smooth curve through the snapshots (Catmull-Rom) instead of straight lines between them. Physics (speeds, acceleration, braking) is unchanged.
- A player leaving (socket closed) despawns at once; their position is saved.
- **The load circle (owner, 2026-10-13; 1,300 m since 2026-10-14):** the server sends each player only the players, Build pieces and blocks inside it; everything beyond isn't sent at all. **It ends where the distance haze turns solid** (owner, 2026-10-14: the 700 m leash circle + 200 m + 400 m), so anything inside it is already loaded while still hidden in the haze and nothing pops in on screen. Objects come and go as ships move (checked twice a second; an object counts as inside when any part of its colliders is); a changed piece is sent to whoever has it or can now reach it. (Its ring shows on the minimap only; the ground ring was removed, 2026-10-14.) A ship that enters is placed where it is (no slide from its last known spot); one that leaves is hidden. Chat, joins and leaves still reach everyone. `LOAD_R` in world3d.js, sent in the hello; keep it equal to the client's `FAR_OUT`.

## Sides and the minimap (owner, 2026-10-13)

- **Sides: Red or Blue, remembered per account** (owner, 2026-10-13). The first time, a player picks one ("Choose a side", two buttons over the world); after that **the game re-joins the remembered side by itself**. **A "Reset faction" checkbox under Enter System on the Welcome back page** brings the picker back for that entry (`game.html?reset=1`, dropped from the address once picked, so reconnects re-join). The side is saved in `world3d.team` when the player joins and sent back in the hello (`side`); the player joins only once a side is sent (`{t:"side", s}`, once per session, only "red" or "blue").
- **The minimap:** the map's hexagon at the bottom left, **always north up**. It shows **your load circle** and **every ship inside it as an arrow pointing the way the ship faces** (owner, 2026-10-13): friendlies (your side) blue, enemies red; yours is blue with a white outline, plus a short white tick for where the camera faces. Ships outside your load circle aren't shown (they aren't sent). Redrawn ten times a second. **Scrolling over the minimap zooms it** (owner, 2026-10-13), not the game: from the whole map in until your load circle just fits inside, **your ship always at its center unless fully zoomed out** (owner); kept per browser. **The minimap keeps its hexagon shape at every zoom** (the map is drawn inside the hexagon frame; zoomed in, the map's own border shows as a **gray hexagon inside the blue frame** where it's in view, so you can tell when you're near the edge (owner)).
- Ships themselves look the same for both sides for now.
- PTR only: `game.html?as=Name` connects as another account (for testing several players locally).

## Controls (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **Ship-relative controls (owner, 2026-10-14): W thrusts the way the ship faces, S flies it backward, A and D turn it**; **backing up, A and D swap** (owner, 2026-10-14: like a car in reverse, backing up with A swings the ship back toward its left) (left / right at 0.5 rad/s, owner 2026-10-14: cut by 75% twice; a full turn takes about 12.5 s; spinning up and easing off over about 0.2 s). Backing up runs at the side / back rate (60%) with the reduced thrust for pushing against the nose, and can't sprint. (Was: WASD moved the ship relative to the camera, with the nose turning to face the push; the reversal-through-the-middle rule went with it.)
- **The middle mouse button, dragged, rotates the camera around the player** — **inverted** (owner, 2026-10-12): dragging right turns the view right. On touch, a one-finger drag does the same (mobile parity: drag = drag). *Open: how movement itself works on touch — nothing is decided yet.*
- **The camera orbits the player looking down toward it** at pitch 60°, rotation 60% (owner: "60d: 600-1800m: 60%"), **600 m out at most; the mouse wheel (touch: pinch) zooms in to 200 m** (owner, 2026-10-13; it was 600–1,800 m, then fixed at 600). **Starting to aim eases the zoom back to 600 m** (owner, 2026-10-13: smoothly, not a snap; about half a second), and the wheel does nothing while aiming; after aiming it stays at 600 until zoomed in again. Aiming still pulls the camera out as described below. No settings panel (owner, 2026-10-13: removed with the weapons); the leash and overflow use fixed values.
- **Right-click aiming (owner, 2026-10-13: weapons and the rest of aiming scrapped, the leash camera brought back):** holding the right button draws a **dashed white line from the ship's center on the ground plane** (owner: not the model's center) **to the reticle, fading out over its last 40%** (owner, 2026-10-13: no dot at the end any more; the dashes scale with zoom). **If a block or another ship is in the way (owner, 2026-10-13), the line is white up to where it would hit and red from there to the reticle** (no fade). **The reticle is the ground point under the pointer, clamped to the blue camera leash circle** (700 m around the ship; shown only with Weapon helpers on): push past it and the reticle rides its edge. The ship doesn't turn toward it (owner, 2026-10-14: the reticle is independent of the ship).
  - **The camera moves toward the reticle** (as before the scrap): driven only by the pointer's screen position and constants, never by the reticle's ground distance (that looped). **A dead zone around the screen's center**: within 30% of the way to the screen's bound nothing moves, easing in to full at 80%. At full press the zoom and the leash (the focus sliding out along the aim) are solved together so the screen edge sits the **overflow** past the circle in the aimed direction — 100 m plus **N S E W +200, diagonals 0**, blended smoothly — with the ship and an 80 m margin kept inside the opposite edge; never under the wheel zoom, capped at 6,000 m. Releasing eases everything back.
  - **The camera trail** (owner, 2026-10-13): while the ship moves and the aim isn't steering the camera (not aiming, or the pointer inside the dead zone), the camera trails **behind the ship along its motion, 0–80 m with speed** (owner, 2026-10-13: was up to 200; 0.4 m per m/s, so 80 m at full speed, capped there when sprinting), like a camera lagging behind (the ship sits ahead of the screen center). It fades out as the aim takes over past the dead zone, and eases back when the ship stops. Not while the middle mouse holds the frame.
  - The reticle is re-projected after the camera takes its final pose each frame, so it never trails the pointer.
  - **Middle mouse while aiming:** the camera frame (zoom and the focus's offset) freezes and orbits the player with the yaw; the cursor is pointer-locked at the press spot and the reticle stays raycast from that screen spot; lifting the middle button frees the cursor.
- **Materializing after a respawn (owner, 2026-10-14):** a ship that respawns (destroyed, or an admin kill / respawn; not joining) appears as a cyan hologram revealed **from its nose to its tail** (the first 60% of 3 s, a bright line at the reveal's edge, drifting scan bands), then **solidifies** into the real ship over the rest. Until it's done it can't move, turn or fire, and it takes no damage. The server keeps the timer (sent with the snapshots as the time left); everyone near sees it.
- **Weapons and the Weapon panel (owner, 2026-10-14; staff: Nyssary and admins):** weapons are kept on the server (table `world3d_weapons`; the Railgun is made the first time, with the numbers below). **Z** (or the **[Z]** button left of [X]) opens the panel (dragged by its top bar, owner 2026-10-14): **Weapon** (a dropdown: "New weapon", then each weapon), **Name** (renames it in the dropdown as typed), **Type** (bullet, for now), **Optimal Range** (m: full damage out to it; the aim line's length), **Falloff Rate** (% of the damage lost per 100 m past the optimal range), **Lifetime** (s before a bullet's gone), **Speed** (m/s), **Shot Rate** (s between shots), **Damage** (per hit), **Default Spread** (the cone's least half-angle, degrees), **Spread Per Shot** (degrees more each way per shot), **Aim Speed** (degrees a second the cone narrows back), **Rotation Speed** (degrees a second the aim line turns toward the pointer; shots go along the aim line, not straight at the pointer), **Reload Speed** (s a reload takes), **Magazine** (shots before a reload); **R reloads** (owner, 2026-10-14): an empty magazine just stops firing until R is pressed (R also tops up a part-empty one; not while the Build menu is open, where R turns pieces); **CANCEL / CHANGE** (CREATE when "New weapon" is picked: the name and fields make a new one; its numbers start at 0, owner 2026-10-14). **ENTER in a field applies it** (as CHANGE / CREATE), **Defaults while the panel is open (owner, 2026-10-14):** each weapon's stats when the panel opened are its defaults; a field that differs from them (typed or saved) is **outlined green if the change is better, red if worse, white if neither** (name, type). Better: more optimal range, lifetime, speed, damage, aim speed, rotation speed and magazine; less falloff, shot rate (seconds between shots), default spread, spread per shot and reload time. **REVERT** (between CANCEL and CHANGE / CREATE) puts the weapon back to its defaults; a weapon made while the panel is open is deleted again (the first weapon can never be deleted). **Closing the panel makes the stats as they are the new defaults.** CHANGE / CREATE is blue; all three buttons have a hover state (owner, 2026-10-14). **The panel stays open after saving**, showing the weapon as saved (the server's limits may have raised a 0, e.g. speed, shot rate, rotation speed and magazine have minimums). Saving changes it for everyone who has it and **equips it on my ship**; everyone else has the first weapon (the Railgun), test ships too. The server checks every field (limits) and keeps the shot rate, magazine, reload, spread and damage; the client mirrors them for the aim line and cone.
- **Railgun (owner, 2026-10-14): left click fires** a fast projectile (owner: a projectile, not a beam; 700 m/s) from my ship's pivot toward the ground point under the pointer, up to 1,000 m, **at most every half second** (owner, 2026-10-14; was once a second; the server keeps the cooldown). **Spread (owner, 2026-10-14):** each shot widens the shooter's cone by 1.5° each way (owner, 2026-10-14: was 3°) and it narrows back at 3° a second (45° at most); a shot leaves at a random angle inside the cone as it was, chosen by the server (test ships too). **While aiming, the aim line is drawn inside that cone** (a faint white wedge with edge lines, back to a line at 0). The server flies it a step each tick: the first block, Build piece (Aim Bounds; clouds and test-ship pieces don't count) or ship (the ship kind's Aim Bounds parts, turned with its heading; test ships included) on the step stops it, and a ship hit takes **25 damage** (the server notes which Aim Bounds part was hit; for now every part takes plain HP damage) (health 100; at 0 it's back at full health near the center, a test ship heals in place). **A ship the shooter can't see by line of sight** (mutual visibility from the Visibility points, blocks and Build pieces only, clouds left out; below 25%, as on screen) **isn't hit: the projectile flies through it**, no damage and no sparks (owner, 2026-10-14). Everyone whose load circle is within reach sees the glowing slug with a short trail; sparks on a ship it hits, a small puff on a wall. A ship I hit shows in a cloud as if it moved (3 s). Not while the Build menu or Object Editor is open, or while typing. The client sends only the direction; the server checks it as hostile (finite, not zero).
- **Weapon helpers** (owner, 2026-10-13: "make a toggle for weapon ui helpers"): a checkbox at the top right. **Off by default**; for now it shows or hides the blue leash circle. Kept per browser (`atamus.helpers`); hidden on touch.
- **The cursor** (owner, 2026-10-13): the system cursor is always hidden on the game page; **a small white dot** (4 px, faint dark outline) sits wherever the pointer is, over the UI too (buttons, the chat, the side picker). It stays put while the middle mouse holds the pointer lock and disappears when the pointer leaves the window. Not on touch.
- **The browser's own right-click menu never opens on the game page**, over the UI either (owner, 2026-10-13); only the game's own menus (a name in chat) open.
- **Touch devices (owner, 2026-10-13: "this will be a PC game, just using mobile to test as a second account"):** a **joystick** at the bottom left drives movement like WASD (analog, small dead zone; forward = where the camera faces); **dragging anywhere else turns the camera**, two fingers on the world pinch the zoom. The minimap moves to the top left. **Right-click has no touch version, by the owner's decision** (an exception to mobile parity), and there's no sprint on touch.

## Chat (owner, 2026-10-13, Foxhole-style)

- **At the bottom right**; **only the lines and the input sit on a soft panel** — the tabs above it, the [A] button on its left and the +/− float (owner): **tabs on top — All | World | Faction | Whisper** (which lines show) with a **+ / − at the far right of the tab row** (no background, thick; owner: collapses the lines to 2 rows, or back to 10; kept per browser), the lines (10 rows, sitting at the bottom), then the input bar with its label **"[Type] Name:"** on the left (a dark well, not white). Lines are tagged **[World] (gray)**, **[Faction] (the speaker's side color)**, **[Whisper] / [To <name>] (purple)** (owner); the last 200 are kept. A tab that gets a line while another is shown is marked (amber); on All nothing is marked.
- **The tab is what you read; the channel is where you type** (owner). **While typing, TAB switches the channel** — World → Faction → Whisper (to the last person whispered, either way) → World — **without changing the tab**, so you can read World and type to Faction. Picking a tab also points the channel at it (All leaves it; Whisper only once there's someone to whisper).
- **All whispers share the Whisper tab** (owner). Receiving or sending a whisper makes that person the one Whisper sends to; sending one switches your channel to Whisper.
- **Enter** opens the input and sends (on a phone, the keyboard's Go / Send key too); Escape or an empty line closes it. Game keys are ignored while typing.
