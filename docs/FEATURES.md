# Atamus: how the game works today

This is a living spec of the game. Read it before every patch, and update it in the same commit as any change to behavior. Rules marked **(owner)** were decided explicitly by the owner, so keep them unless the owner says otherwise.

## The 3D rebuild (owner, 2026-10-12)

The owner wiped the 2D game to rebuild Atamus in 3D. **The whole 2D game — its world, client, UI, modules, combat, ETA, market, pilots, licenses and the rest — lives on the `legacy-2d` branch** (tip `e56e61d`); bring pieces back from there only when the owner asks. The accounts backend (auth, sessions, security, the admin API, the deploy countdown) was kept on `main` and still runs; the admin panel and chat have no frontend right now.

**Old data is kept (owner):** accounts, credits, pilots, licenses and the old game tables stay in the database untouched for reference. The 3D game only reads and writes `users` (auth) and its own `world3d` table.

## The world (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **One shared 3D world on one server: no POIs, no systems, no instances.** Each player is sent only what is inside their **load circle** (below).
- A flat ground plane with a **2D grid** (100 m cells) drawn on it.
- **The border is the same flat-top hexagon** (flat edges top and bottom, points east and west), **126 km flat to flat** (owner, 2026-10-12; was 5 miles), drawn as a glowing band on the ground. It stops players: movement walks as far along the step as still fits, and the sphere's edge rests on the line.
- **Each player is one ship** (owner, 2026-10-12: the owner's `Combat_Cruiser.glb`, Y up, +Z forward, in `website/models/`), **scaled to 100 m long**, resting on the grid and **turning to face the way it travels at a fast fixed rate** (owner: "almost instantly, but not instantly"; **8 rad/s**, owner 2026-10-13: first 12, then 4, then doubled to 8; a U-turn takes about 0.4 s), pivoting about the hull's middle. **Reversals turn through the last middle direction** (owner, 2026-10-13): when the WASD direction flips to (nearly) the opposite one, the nose turns the way round that passes through the direction used before the last ordinary change — going A, W, D and then D, A, D, A… it swings over the top (W) every time instead of whichever way happens to be shortest. Ordinary (non-reversing) changes still take the short way; aiming always does. The model's nose is -Z, flipped on load. **The ship faces the way WASD pushes it; while aiming (right button held, with or without the middle button) it turns toward the reticle instead, even while moving** (owner, 2026-10-13). The heading also sets how much thrust a push gets (see Weight), and others see it through the synced heading. **With neither aiming nor WASD, it keeps the last direction it faced** (owner, 2026-10-12): only held input re-aims your ship, so network-correction nudges never turn it; other ships re-aim only at a sustained real speed. Everyone flies the same ship for now. One per account; it spawns within 300 m of the center the first time and comes back where it was after that (positions saved every 10 s and on leaving; a saved position outside the hexagon respawns at the center). Until the model loads, a blue sphere stands in. The server still treats players as 50 m circles for the border.
- **Travel is on the ground plane only (XY): no movement up or down on Z.**
- **Speed: 200 m/s forward** (owner, 2026-10-13; was 300; `SPEED` in `world3d.js`). Crossing the map takes about 10½ minutes. **Sideways and backward the top speed is 60% (120 m/s)** (owner, 2026-10-13), blended in between (a diagonal push about 88%). **What counts as forward** (owner, 2026-10-13): **not aiming, every direction is forward** (WASD turns the ship that way), so it's always full speed; **aiming, forward is toward the reticle**, so strafing and backing off are slower. The client sends the reticle's direction with each move while aiming (`{t:"move", …, a}`, wrapped; absent when not aiming). The acceleration share (above) still follows the actual nose. (`SIDE_SPEED` in world3d.js, sent in the hello.)
- **Weight (owner, 2026-10-13):** ships have momentum. Thrust accelerates at **200 m/s²** toward the input's velocity (0 to 200 m/s in about 1 s straight), but **a push off the nose gets only part of it**: full when the nose points where it's pushed, about half side-on, **30% pushing against the nose** (it has to come round first). **Letting go of WASD brakes at least 400 m/s²**, however the nose points, and **a stop always ends within 100 m, whatever the speed** (owner, 2026-10-13): the braking rate is fixed when WASD is let go at the harder of 400 m/s² and the rate that stops the current speed in 100 m (at today's speeds the 400 m/s² floor decides: 200 m/s stops in 50 m / 0.5 s, a 260 m/s sprint in about 85 m / 0.65 s; the 100 m cap only takes over above about 283 m/s). Pressing WASD again ends the stop. (`STOP_D` in world3d.js, sent in the hello.) Online, the ship also covers the distance flown before the server hears the release (half a round trip). The nose turns toward the **input** direction, not the drift. **Changing direction while moving brakes the old motion at the braking rate** (owner, 2026-10-13: reversing was far slower than stopping and going): the part of the velocity change that slows the current motion uses at least 400 m/s², the rest (the new direction) the thrust above, so reversing takes about as long as letting go and pushing the other way (200 m/s forward to 200 m/s back in about 1.5 s), and turns bleed the old drift fast. Server and client integrate the same way (`ACCEL`, `BRAKE`, `TURN_ACCEL` in world3d.js, sent in the hello); hitting the border still stops the ship dead.
- **The capacitor (owner, 2026-10-13):** a 0–100 bar at the bottom center. **Sprinting drains it** (20 a second), **moving without sprinting recharges it slowly** (4 a second), **sitting still faster** (12 a second). Run dry and sprint stays off until it's back to 15. The server enforces it (each player is sent their own charge 4 times a second, to them only) and the client predicts it the same way.
- **The stop marker (owner, 2026-10-13):** while the ship is moving, a light-blue dashed line runs to a small flat dot (2 m radius at close zoom, like the aim dot) where it would come to rest if the player let go now (its velocity at the braking rate, so never more than 100 m out). Only the player sees their own.
- **Sprint (owner, 2026-10-12): holding Shift makes the ship 30% faster** (owner, 2026-10-13; now 260 m/s, was double; `SPRINT` in world3d.js), **forward only** (owner, 2026-10-13: within 45° of forward — any direction when not aiming, toward the reticle when aiming; otherwise Shift does nothing and the capacitor isn't drained) — **but not while aiming** (owner, 2026-10-13: the right button held turns sprint off; the client sends no sprint flag then). The sprint is just a flag to the server (`{t:"move", x, y, b}`); the server applies the speed-up. *Open: its touch equivalent.*
- **X drops a cube** (owner, 2026-10-12: a movement reference "for now"): a 50 m cube at the player's position, local to this browser (others don't see it; the newest 200 are kept; gone on reload).
- The grid is drawn by the ground's own shader (anti-aliased 100 m lines on the surface itself), after a separate layer of rendered lines flickered against the ground.
- **Server-authoritative:** the client only sends a direction and its ship's heading (`{t:"move", x, y, b, h}`, direction clamped to length 1, heading wrapped); **after movement or rotation stops, the settled state is sent twice more** (owner, 2026-10-13) so the server and everyone else land on the real final position and heading. Other screens draw each ship with the heading its owner sent (snapshots carry `[id, x, y, heading]`); the server simulates at 20 Hz and sends each client its own snapshot at 15 Hz (only the ships in its load circle). The client predicts its own sphere locally (with the same border rule) and is softly corrected by snapshots; other ships keep a short history of snapshot positions timed by the server's clock and are drawn 150 ms in the past, interpolated between the two snapshots around that moment (briefly carried on along their last motion if a snapshot is late); they turn only on frames where they really moved.
- A player leaving (socket closed) despawns at once; their position is saved.
- **The load circle (owner, 2026-10-13): 5,000 m around each ship.** The server sends each player only the players inside it (and, later, objects); everything beyond it isn't sent at all. It reaches well past the camera, so nothing pops in on screen. **It is drawn for now** (owner): a thin gray-blue ring on the ground around your ship, and on the minimap. A ship that enters is placed where it is (no slide from its last known spot); one that leaves is hidden. Chat, joins and leaves still reach everyone. `LOAD_R` in world3d.js, sent in the hello.

## Sides and the minimap (owner, 2026-10-13)

- **Every time a player enters the game they choose a side: Red or Blue** ("Choose a side", two buttons over the world). They join the world only once they've picked; the server keeps the side for the session (`{t:"side", s}`, once per session, only "red" or "blue"). Reconnecting asks again.
- **The minimap:** the map's hexagon at the bottom left, **always north up**. It shows **your load circle** and **every ship inside it as an arrow pointing the way the ship faces** (owner, 2026-10-13): friendlies (your side) blue, enemies red; yours is blue with a white outline, plus a short white tick for where the camera faces. Ships outside your load circle aren't shown (they aren't sent). Redrawn ten times a second.
- Ships themselves look the same for both sides for now.
- PTR only: `game.html?as=Name` connects as another account (for testing several players locally).

## Controls (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **WASD moves the sphere; wherever the camera is facing is "forward".**
- **The middle mouse button, dragged, rotates the camera around the player** — **inverted** (owner, 2026-10-12): dragging right turns the view right. On touch, a one-finger drag does the same (mobile parity: drag = drag). *Open: how movement itself works on touch — nothing is decided yet.*
- **The camera orbits the player looking down toward it** at pitch 60°, rotation 60% (owner: "60d: 600-1800m: 60%"), **fixed at 600 m: the max zoom equals the min zoom** (owner, 2026-10-13; was 600–1,800 m), so the mouse wheel and pinch no longer zoom. Aiming still pulls the camera out as described below. No settings panel (owner, 2026-10-13: removed with the weapons); the leash and overflow use fixed values.
- **Right-click aiming (owner, 2026-10-13: weapons and the rest of aiming scrapped, the leash camera brought back):** holding the right button draws a **dashed white line from the ship's center on the ground plane** (owner: not the model's center) **to the reticle, fading out over its last 40%** (owner, 2026-10-13: no dot at the end any more; the dashes scale with zoom). **The reticle is the ground point under the pointer, clamped to the blue camera leash circle** (700 m around the ship; shown only with Weapon helpers on): push past it and the reticle rides its edge. The ship turns to face it (see the world section).
  - **The camera moves toward the reticle** (as before the scrap): driven only by the pointer's screen position and constants, never by the reticle's ground distance (that looped). **A dead zone around the screen's center**: within 30% of the way to the screen's bound nothing moves, easing in to full at 80%. At full press the zoom and the leash (the focus sliding out along the aim) are solved together so the screen edge sits the **overflow** past the circle in the aimed direction — 100 m plus **N S E W +200, diagonals 0**, blended smoothly — with the ship and an 80 m margin kept inside the opposite edge; never under the wheel zoom, capped at 6,000 m. Releasing eases everything back.
  - The reticle is re-projected after the camera takes its final pose each frame, so it never trails the pointer.
  - **Middle mouse while aiming:** the camera frame (zoom and the focus's offset) freezes and orbits the player with the yaw; the cursor is pointer-locked at the press spot and the reticle stays raycast from that screen spot; lifting the middle button frees the cursor.
- **Weapon helpers** (owner, 2026-10-13: "make a toggle for weapon ui helpers"): a checkbox at the top right. **Off by default**; for now it shows or hides the blue leash circle. Kept per browser (`atamus.helpers`); hidden on touch.
- **The cursor** (owner, 2026-10-13): the system cursor is always hidden on the game page; **a small white dot** (4 px, faint dark outline) sits wherever the pointer is, over the UI too (buttons, the chat, the side picker). It stays put while the middle mouse holds the pointer lock and disappears when the pointer leaves the window. Not on touch.
- The browser's own right-click menu never opens in the game (kept from before).
- **Touch devices (owner, 2026-10-13: "this will be a PC game, just using mobile to test as a second account"):** a **joystick** at the bottom left drives movement like WASD (analog, small dead zone; forward = where the camera faces); **dragging anywhere else turns the camera**, two fingers on the world pinch the zoom. The minimap moves to the top left. **Right-click has no touch version, by the owner's decision** (an exception to mobile parity), and there's no sprint on touch.

## Chat (owner, 2026-10-13)

- **Global** (everyone) and **Faction** (your side) tabs above the minimap. **Enter** opens the box and sends; Escape or an empty line closes it. Simple: messages go through as typed (240 characters at most), shown as plain text with the speaker's name in their side's color. No history: you see what's said while you're online. Game keys are ignored while typing (in chat or in the editor's fields).

## Accounts and sessions

Kept from before, unchanged (`auth.js`, `sessions.js`, `security.js` and the rest): sign-up with the EULA, log in / log out, Remember Password, password recovery and reset requests, device ids, failed-login backoff, impossible-travel locks, bans and the session cookie. **One session per account:** a new tab or device replaces the old one (close 4002: "Playing on another tab or device"). A dead session (4001) goes back to the login page.

- **The website** (atamus.io) is unchanged except play.html: **no more character or pilot creation** — a logged-in account goes straight to **Enter System**. The front page, login, signup, recovery and the EULA are as they were. The pixel logo and icons stay.
- The admin panel's API, moderation, appeals, tickets, the name filter and the trade-watch tables still exist server-side but have no frontend; admin hooks into the game world are disconnected (kicks and freezes don't reach the 3D world yet).

## Live updates (kept)

- Every push to `main` warns every connected player ahead of the switch — **1 s for now (owner, 2026-10-12: "make countdown 1 second for now"**; it was 30 s and goes back up when the owner says): the game shows **"Update incoming — N s"** top center, then sends them to `play.html?updating=1`, which waits for the new build and offers Enter System again. The length lives in `gamehttp.js` (`announceUpdate(part, 1)`) and the `sleep` in both deploy workflows.
- The deploy workflows are unchanged (`deploy-website.yml` on `website/**`, `deploy-server.yml` on `packages/server/**`).
