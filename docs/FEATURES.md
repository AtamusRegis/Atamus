# Atamus: how the game works today

This is a living spec of the game. Read it before every patch, and update it in the same commit as any change to behavior. Rules marked **(owner)** were decided explicitly by the owner, so keep them unless the owner says otherwise.

## The 3D rebuild (owner, 2026-10-12)

The owner wiped the 2D game to rebuild Atamus in 3D. **The whole 2D game — its world, client, UI, modules, combat, ETA, market, pilots, licenses and the rest — lives on the `legacy-2d` branch** (tip `e56e61d`); bring pieces back from there only when the owner asks. The accounts backend (auth, sessions, security, the admin API, the deploy countdown) was kept on `main` and still runs; the admin panel and chat have no frontend right now.

**Old data is kept (owner):** accounts, credits, pilots, licenses and the old game tables stay in the database untouched for reference. The 3D game only reads and writes `users` (auth) and its own `world3d` table.

## The world (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **One shared 3D world on one server: no POIs, no systems, no instances.** Each player is sent only what is inside their **load circle** (below).
- A flat ground plane with a **2D grid** (100 m cells) drawn on it. **Colors (owner, 2026-10-13, from a reference image):** a dark charcoal ground and background (#18181e) with soft gray lines (about #3f3f45 at their brightest); the far ground fades into the same charcoal. The grid shader outputs in sRGB, so the colors show as specified.
- **The border is the same flat-top hexagon** (flat edges top and bottom, points east and west), **2,500 m flat to flat for now** (owner, 2026-10-13; it was 126 km, and 5 miles before that), drawn as a glowing band on the ground. It stops players: movement walks as far along the step as still fits, and the sphere's edge rests on the line.
- **Each player is one ship** (owner, 2026-10-12: the owner's `Combat_Cruiser.glb`, Y up, +Z forward, in `website/models/`), **scaled to 100 m long**, resting on the grid and **turning to face the way it travels at a fast fixed rate** (owner: "almost instantly, but not instantly"; **8 rad/s**, owner 2026-10-13: first 12, then 4, then doubled to 8; a U-turn takes about 0.4 s), pivoting about the hull's middle. **Reversals turn through the last middle direction** (owner, 2026-10-13): when the WASD direction flips to (nearly) the opposite one, the nose turns the way round that passes through the direction used before the last ordinary change — going A, W, D and then D, A, D, A… it swings over the top (W) every time instead of whichever way happens to be shortest. Ordinary (non-reversing) changes still take the short way; aiming always does. The model's nose is -Z, flipped on load. **The ship faces the way WASD pushes it; while aiming (right button held, with or without the middle button) it turns toward the reticle instead, even while moving** (owner, 2026-10-13). The heading also sets how much thrust a push gets (see Weight), and others see it through the synced heading. **With neither aiming nor WASD, it keeps the last direction it faced** (owner, 2026-10-12): only held input re-aims your ship, so network-correction nudges never turn it; other ships re-aim only at a sustained real speed. Everyone flies the same ship for now. One per account; it spawns within 300 m of the center the first time and comes back where it was after that — unless **Reset location** is ticked under Enter System on the Welcome back page (owner, 2026-10-13), which puts it back within 300 m of the center for that entry (`game.html?resetpos=1`, sent with the side; dropped from the address once used, so reconnects don't reset again) (positions saved every 10 s and on leaving; a saved position outside the hexagon respawns at the center). Until the model loads, a blue sphere stands in. The server still treats players as 50 m circles for the border.
- **Travel is on the ground plane only (XY): no movement up or down on Z.**
- **Speed: 200 m/s forward** (owner, 2026-10-13; was 300; `SPEED` in `world3d.js`). Crossing the map takes about 12 seconds at full speed. **Sideways and backward the top speed is 60% (120 m/s)** (owner, 2026-10-13), blended in between (a diagonal push about 88%). **What counts as forward** (owner, 2026-10-13): **not aiming, every direction is forward** (WASD turns the ship that way), so it's always full speed; **aiming, forward is toward the reticle**, so strafing and backing off are slower. The client sends the reticle's direction with each move while aiming (`{t:"move", …, a}`, wrapped; absent when not aiming). The acceleration share (above) still follows the actual nose. (`SIDE_SPEED` in world3d.js, sent in the hello.)
- **Weight (owner, 2026-10-13):** ships have momentum. Thrust accelerates at **200 m/s²** toward the input's velocity (0 to 200 m/s in about 1 s straight), but **a push off the nose gets only part of it**: full when the nose points where it's pushed, about half side-on, **30% pushing against the nose** (it has to come round first). **Letting go of WASD brakes at least 400 m/s²**, however the nose points, and **a stop always ends within 100 m, whatever the speed** (owner, 2026-10-13): the braking rate is fixed when WASD is let go at the harder of 400 m/s² and the rate that stops the current speed in 100 m (at today's speeds the 400 m/s² floor decides: 200 m/s stops in 50 m / 0.5 s, a 260 m/s sprint in about 85 m / 0.65 s; the 100 m cap only takes over above about 283 m/s). Pressing WASD again ends the stop. (`STOP_D` in world3d.js, sent in the hello.) Online, the ship also covers the distance flown before the server hears the release (half a round trip). The nose turns toward the **input** direction, not the drift. **Changing direction keeps the momentum** (owner, 2026-10-13: "not lose speed and regain it, just keep momentum in the new direction with a quick rotation"): while WASD is held, the ship's motion keeps its speed and swings toward the new direction at 8 rad/s, the nose's own turn rate (a 90° change takes about 0.2 s, a reversal about 0.4 s as a tight U-turn, swinging round the side the nose turns to). Its speed meanwhile eases toward the target speed for that direction: up at the thrust above (scaled by how far the push is off the nose), down at the braking rate (e.g. when aiming makes strafing 60%). From rest it sets off straight along the input. (`carry` / `VEL_TURN` in world3d.js, mirrored in the client.) Server and client integrate the same way (`ACCEL`, `BRAKE`, `TURN_ACCEL` in world3d.js, sent in the hello); hitting the border still stops the ship dead.
- **The capacitor (owner, 2026-10-13):** a 0–100 bar at the bottom center. **Sprinting drains it 1 for every 100 m covered** (owner, 2026-10-13; was 20 a second: a full bar is 10 km of sprint, about 38 s at 260 m/s), **moving without sprinting recharges it slowly** (4 a second), **sitting still faster** (12 a second). Run dry and sprint stays off until it's back to 15. The server enforces it (each player is sent their own charge 4 times a second, to them only) and the client predicts it the same way.
- **The stop marker (owner, 2026-10-13):** while the ship is moving, a light-blue dashed line runs to a small flat dot (2 m radius at close zoom, like the aim dot) where it would come to rest if the player let go now (its velocity at the braking rate, so never more than 100 m out). Only the player sees their own.
- **Sprint (owner, 2026-10-12): holding Shift makes the ship 30% faster** (owner, 2026-10-13; now 260 m/s, was double; `SPRINT` in world3d.js), **forward only** (owner, 2026-10-13: within 45° of forward — any direction when not aiming, toward the reticle when aiming; otherwise Shift does nothing and the capacitor isn't drained) — **but not while aiming** (owner, 2026-10-13: the right button held turns sprint off; the client sends no sprint flag then). The sprint is just a flag to the server (`{t:"move", x, y, b}`); the server applies the speed-up. *Open: its touch equivalent.*
- **X drops a block** (owner, 2026-10-12: a movement reference "for now"; shared, 2026-10-13): a 50 m block at the player's position, kept by the server and seen by everyone (30 per player, the oldest goes; 400 in all; gone when the server restarts). The ship that dropped it is pushed out of it. **For staff, X opens the Object Editor instead** (owner, 2026-10-14).
- **Colliders (owner, 2026-10-13):** on the ground plane only (height ignored). Ships can't pass through blocks (50 m squares), Build pieces or each other: they stop or slide along (two ships that meet are each pushed half the overlap apart). Each ship collides by the ship's Movement ellipse (Object Editor; it starts as a 45 m circle), pieces by their kind's Movement ellipse. The server decides; the client predicts the same pushes for its own ship.
- **Space under the grid (owner, 2026-10-14):** the field is only its grid lines; under it is a deep blue nebula (from the owner's references) in parallax layers: furthest down a dark navy base with marbled blue clouds (swirled noise), dark dust lanes and large darker regions (owner: less saturated), then a layer of cyan glow spots and a nearer layer of faint blue wisps, each at its own depth so they slide past each other as you move; it drifts very slowly on its own. Between it and the grid is a field of very thin stars (white, blue, cyan, a few gold; a few bigger ones with a glow) at varied depths, so they parallax against the grid; each slowly dims in and out on its own. (Replaces the dark charcoal ground.)
- **Rendering for speed (owner, 2026-10-14):** the cloud noise is worked out once at start into a seamless 1024 × 1024 texture that the nebula only looks up; the three nebula layers are one plane (each pixel finds where its line of sight crosses each layer's depth); the grid lines are drawn in the same pass as the haze; nebula pixels behind the solid haze are skipped; the line-of-sight shadow texture is redrawn only when your ship has moved or a block / Build piece changed; and the game draws at most 1.5 pixels per screen pixel.
- **Line of sight (owner, 2026-10-13):** on the ground plane (height ignored, like the colliders). Blocks and Build pieces cast shadows from their Sight outlines, from their far side only (the object and its footprint are never shaded); ships cast none. **Mutual visibility (owner, 2026-10-14):** if a player can see you, you can see them, and the less of each other you can see, the more see-through you are to each other. The pair's visibility, the same both ways: **the line between the two pivots counts for 20%** (owner, 2026-10-14; was half); for the other 80%, each ship's **Visibility points** (Object Editor) send a line to the other ship's pivot, and the share of those not cut by a block or Build piece counts. Each other ship (and test ship) is drawn whole, its opacity **rising smoothly from 0 at 25% visible to solid at 75%** (owner, 2026-10-14; no steps), **fading in fast (about 0.15 s) and out slowly (about 0.6 s)** so it shows promptly but doesn't flicker at corners. The minimap arrows follow the same opacity. Worked out 30 times a second. **With the Object Editor open** each other ship's lines to my pivot are drawn, plus the pivot-to-pivot line (mine to them aren't drawn, owner 2026-10-14; clear green, blocked red) with the percentage over each ship, for testing. Client-side only for now: the server still sends every ship in the load circle.
- **Unseen ground is shaded (owner, 2026-10-13):** the shadow areas, and everything past the map's edge, get a flat, see-through black layer over the grid (owner, 2026-10-14: was a drifting dusty blue / purple texture). Shadow edges are slightly blurred so they aren't stepped. The map's border band is drawn over it (owner, 2026-10-14), so the edge always shows.
- **Sight fades with distance (owner, 2026-10-14):** starting 200 m past the leash circle (900 m from my ship) the haze ramps up from clear to fully solid 400 m further out (1,300 m). Nothing past 1,300 m is drawn (other ships, test ships, blocks, Build pieces), and the minimap leaves out ships past it too.
- The grid is drawn by the ground's own shader (anti-aliased 100 m lines on the surface itself), after a separate layer of rendered lines flickered against the ground.
- **Server-authoritative:** the client only sends a direction and its ship's heading (`{t:"move", x, y, b, h}`, direction clamped to length 1, heading wrapped); **after movement or rotation stops, the settled state is sent twice more** (owner, 2026-10-13) so the server and everyone else land on the real final position and heading. Other screens draw each ship with the heading its owner sent (snapshots carry `[id, x, y, heading]`); the server simulates at 20 Hz and sends each client its own snapshot at 15 Hz (only the ships in its load circle). The client predicts its own sphere locally (with the same border rule) and is softly corrected by snapshots; other ships keep a short history of snapshot positions timed by the server's clock and are drawn 150 ms in the past, interpolated between the two snapshots around that moment (briefly carried on along their last motion if a snapshot is late); they turn only on frames where they really moved.
- A player leaving (socket closed) despawns at once; their position is saved.
- **The load circle (owner, 2026-10-13; 1,300 m since 2026-10-14):** the server sends each player only the players, Build pieces and blocks inside it; everything beyond isn't sent at all. **It ends where the distance haze turns solid** (owner, 2026-10-14: the 700 m leash circle + 200 m + 400 m), so anything inside it is already loaded while still hidden in the haze and nothing pops in on screen. Objects come and go as ships move (checked twice a second; an object counts as inside when any part of its colliders is); a changed piece is sent to whoever has it or can now reach it. (Its ring shows on the minimap only; the ground ring was removed, 2026-10-14.) A ship that enters is placed where it is (no slide from its last known spot); one that leaves is hidden. Chat, joins and leaves still reach everyone. `LOAD_R` in world3d.js, sent in the hello; keep it equal to the client's `FAR_OUT`.

## Sides and the minimap (owner, 2026-10-13)

- **Sides: Red or Blue, remembered per account** (owner, 2026-10-13). The first time, a player picks one ("Choose a side", two buttons over the world); after that **the game re-joins the remembered side by itself**. **A "Reset faction" checkbox under Enter System on the Welcome back page** brings the picker back for that entry (`game.html?reset=1`, dropped from the address once picked, so reconnects re-join). The side is saved in `world3d.team` when the player joins and sent back in the hello (`side`); the player joins only once a side is sent (`{t:"side", s}`, once per session, only "red" or "blue").
- **The minimap:** the map's hexagon at the bottom left, **always north up**. It shows **your load circle** and **every ship inside it as an arrow pointing the way the ship faces** (owner, 2026-10-13): friendlies (your side) blue, enemies red; yours is blue with a white outline, plus a short white tick for where the camera faces. Ships outside your load circle aren't shown (they aren't sent). Redrawn ten times a second. **Scrolling over the minimap zooms it** (owner, 2026-10-13), not the game: from the whole map in until your load circle just fits inside, **your ship always at its center unless fully zoomed out** (owner); kept per browser. **The minimap keeps its hexagon shape at every zoom** (the map is drawn inside the hexagon frame; zoomed in, the map's own border shows as a **gray hexagon inside the blue frame** where it's in view, so you can tell when you're near the edge (owner)).
- Ships themselves look the same for both sides for now.
- PTR only: `game.html?as=Name` connects as another account (for testing several players locally).

## Controls (owner, 2026-10-12)

- **WASD moves the sphere; wherever the camera is facing is "forward".**
- **The middle mouse button, dragged, rotates the camera around the player** — **inverted** (owner, 2026-10-12): dragging right turns the view right. On touch, a one-finger drag does the same (mobile parity: drag = drag). *Open: how movement itself works on touch — nothing is decided yet.*
- **The camera orbits the player looking down toward it** at pitch 60°, rotation 60% (owner: "60d: 600-1800m: 60%"), **600 m out at most; the mouse wheel (touch: pinch) zooms in to 200 m** (owner, 2026-10-13; it was 600–1,800 m, then fixed at 600). **Starting to aim eases the zoom back to 600 m** (owner, 2026-10-13: smoothly, not a snap; about half a second), and the wheel does nothing while aiming; after aiming it stays at 600 until zoomed in again. Aiming still pulls the camera out as described below. No settings panel (owner, 2026-10-13: removed with the weapons); the leash and overflow use fixed values.
- **Right-click aiming (owner, 2026-10-13: weapons and the rest of aiming scrapped, the leash camera brought back):** holding the right button draws a **dashed white line from the ship's center on the ground plane** (owner: not the model's center) **to the reticle, fading out over its last 40%** (owner, 2026-10-13: no dot at the end any more; the dashes scale with zoom). **If a block or another ship is in the way (owner, 2026-10-13), the line is white up to where it would hit and red from there to the reticle** (no fade). **The reticle is the ground point under the pointer, clamped to the blue camera leash circle** (700 m around the ship; shown only with Weapon helpers on): push past it and the reticle rides its edge. The ship turns to face it (see the world section).
  - **The camera moves toward the reticle** (as before the scrap): driven only by the pointer's screen position and constants, never by the reticle's ground distance (that looped). **A dead zone around the screen's center**: within 30% of the way to the screen's bound nothing moves, easing in to full at 80%. At full press the zoom and the leash (the focus sliding out along the aim) are solved together so the screen edge sits the **overflow** past the circle in the aimed direction — 100 m plus **N S E W +200, diagonals 0**, blended smoothly — with the ship and an 80 m margin kept inside the opposite edge; never under the wheel zoom, capped at 6,000 m. Releasing eases everything back.
  - **The camera trail** (owner, 2026-10-13): while the ship moves and the aim isn't steering the camera (not aiming, or the pointer inside the dead zone), the camera trails **behind the ship along its motion, 0–80 m with speed** (owner, 2026-10-13: was up to 200; 0.4 m per m/s, so 80 m at full speed, capped there when sprinting), like a camera lagging behind (the ship sits ahead of the screen center). It fades out as the aim takes over past the dead zone, and eases back when the ship stops. Not while the middle mouse holds the frame.
  - The reticle is re-projected after the camera takes its final pose each frame, so it never trails the pointer.
  - **Middle mouse while aiming:** the camera frame (zoom and the focus's offset) freezes and orbits the player with the yaw; the cursor is pointer-locked at the press spot and the reticle stays raycast from that screen spot; lifting the middle button frees the cursor.
- **Weapon helpers** (owner, 2026-10-13: "make a toggle for weapon ui helpers"): a checkbox at the top right. **Off by default**; for now it shows or hides the blue leash circle. Kept per browser (`atamus.helpers`); hidden on touch.
- **The cursor** (owner, 2026-10-13): the system cursor is always hidden on the game page; **a small white dot** (4 px, faint dark outline) sits wherever the pointer is, over the UI too (buttons, the chat, the side picker). It stays put while the middle mouse holds the pointer lock and disappears when the pointer leaves the window. Not on touch.
- **The browser's own right-click menu never opens on the game page**, over the UI either (owner, 2026-10-13); only the game's own menus (a name in chat) open.
- **Touch devices (owner, 2026-10-13: "this will be a PC game, just using mobile to test as a second account"):** a **joystick** at the bottom left drives movement like WASD (analog, small dead zone; forward = where the camera faces); **dragging anywhere else turns the camera**, two fingers on the world pinch the zoom. The minimap moves to the top left. **Right-click has no touch version, by the owner's decision** (an exception to mobile parity), and there's no sprint on touch.

## Chat (owner, 2026-10-13, Foxhole-style)

- **At the bottom right**; **only the lines and the input sit on a soft panel** — the tabs above it, the [A] button on its left and the +/− float (owner): **tabs on top — All | World | Faction | Whisper** (which lines show) with a **+ / − at the far right of the tab row** (no background, thick; owner: collapses the lines to 2 rows, or back to 10; kept per browser), the lines (10 rows, sitting at the bottom), then the input bar with its label **"[Type] Name:"** on the left (a dark well, not white). Lines are tagged **[World] (gray)**, **[Faction] (the speaker's side color)**, **[Whisper] / [To <name>] (purple)** (owner); the last 200 are kept. A tab that gets a line while another is shown is marked (amber); on All nothing is marked.
- **The tab is what you read; the channel is where you type** (owner). **While typing, TAB switches the channel** — World → Faction → Whisper (to the last person whispered, either way) → World — **without changing the tab**, so you can read World and type to Faction. Picking a tab also points the channel at it (All leaves it; Whisper only once there's someone to whisper).
- **All whispers share the Whisper tab** (owner). Receiving or sending a whisper makes that person the one Whisper sends to; sending one switches your channel to Whisper.
- **Enter** opens the input and sends (on a phone, the keyboard's Go / Send key too); Escape or an empty line closes it. Game keys are ignored while typing.
- **`/whisper Name message`** (or `/whisper "Name with spaces" message`) whispers an online player by name from anywhere (any case). No one online by that name: "<name> is offline."
- **Right-clicking a name in the chat** (owner): **Whisper** (the channel becomes a whisper to them, ready to type), **Report** (a short note to the admins; the server keeps the reported player's recent lines with it; at most 5 reports in 10 minutes), and **for staff, Admin** (the disciplinary panel). Right-click on ships stays aiming.
- Messages go through as typed (240 characters at most), shown as plain text with the speaker's name in their side's color. A muted player's lines go nowhere. No history: you see what's said while you're online.

## Accounts and sessions

Kept from before (`auth.js`, `sessions.js`, `security.js`): sign-up with the EULA, log in / log out, Remember Password, **password recovery with the three security questions only**, device ids, failed-login backoff (per account and device, in memory), bans (told at login, with ban appeals written from the login page) and the session cookie. **No login records are kept** (owner, 2026-10-13). An account locked in the 2D era stays locked until its recovery questions unlock it. **One session per account:** a new tab or device replaces the old one (close 4002: "Playing on another tab or device"). A dead session (4001) goes back to the login page.
- **One account per device** (owner, kept 2026-10-13): each browser has a device id; while one account is in the game on it, logging in as another account is refused ("Another account is playing on this device."), and a second account's game connection from that device is closed (4003) with the same message.

- **The website** (atamus.io) is unchanged except play.html: **no more character or pilot creation** — a logged-in account goes straight to **Enter System** (with the Reset faction and Reset location checkboxes under it; everything on the Welcome back card is centered, owner 2026-10-13). **The front page (owner, 2026-10-14):** its description is just "WIP prototype of a persistent space warfare game." with Play now; the "Help build Atamus" panel is gone; the Updates list holds only the current (3D) game's entries. Login, signup, recovery and the EULA are as they were. The pixel logo and icons stay.
- **The admin panel** (owner, 2026-10-13): staff accounts (role head / admin / mod; Nyssary is the Head Admin) see an **[A] button just outside the chat input, on its left** (the chat tabs and lines start over the input, not over [A]) (owner, 2026-10-13; hover: Admin); **being logged in as a staff account is enough, no second admin password**. **One window, one size for every section** (owner, 2026-10-13: the Players size — the tabs, the search box and 6 rows), so switching sections doesn't flicker (the new section is drawn off-screen and swapped in when its data arrives); its sections are **floating icon buttons on its left** (names on hover): **Players**, **Reports**, **Appeals**. **Players:** **Online / Offline** tabs, then a **search box** for the selected list (any part of a name), then the list: **6 rows show, then it scrolls** while the tabs and search stay at the top. A row: **name** (up to 20 characters, monospace, in their side's color when online) | **Mute** tag | **Ban** tag | **last played** ("now", or how long ago; from the 3D world's saved positions) | **Discipline** (the disciplinary panel). **Both tags are on every row** (owner, 2026-10-13): **grayed** unless running; **lit while running — Mute yellow, Ban red — with the time left inside, under the word** (∞ for indefinite); grayed, they hold **the total time the player has spent muted / banned** inside (a lifted one counts only until it was lifted; nothing if never; kept in a `discipline` table from 2026-10-13, earlier ones aren't counted). Kicks aren't shown. Offline lists everyone else, most recently played first (200 at most). **Reports:** players' reports (from a name in chat), open first; click one for the note and the reported player's recent lines, with **Discipline** and **Close**. **Appeals:** the ban appeals; open one to read it, reply, and Accept (unbans) or Deny, with an optional note. Admins appear to players as "Admin". Every action goes in the admin log. Hidden on touch. (`admin.js`, `website/adminpanel.js`.)
- **The disciplinary panel** (owner, 2026-10-13: its own window, no reason text boxes), from Discipline or the **Admin** item on a name in chat: **Type** — Kick, Mute, Ban, Kill, Respawn, Teleport to me, Teleport to player, plus **Unmute** / **Unban** when the player is muted / banned; **Reason** (mute: Language, Spam; ban: Language, Spam, Disruptive Behavior); a **Length** slider for mutes and bans with notches **5 min, 30 min, 6 h, 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, Indefinite**; **Add note** (an optional note to the player); **Cancel / Accept**. What the player sees: kicked — "Kicked" (": note") and back to the Welcome back page; banned — "Banned: reason — note", then Log in shows the ban and its appeal; muted — a chat line "Muted <length>: reason — note", and their lines go nowhere; killed or respawned — back near the center, stopped (the two are the same until ships can be destroyed); teleported — stopped, 150 m from the other ship. For the other types a note shows as a line in their chat. Staff can't discipline themselves or other staff (teleports work on anyone but yourself); teleports need both ships in the world.

## Live updates (kept)

- Every push to `main` warns every connected player ahead of the switch — **1 s for now (owner, 2026-10-12: "make countdown 1 second for now"**; it was 30 s and goes back up when the owner says): the game shows **"Update incoming — N s"** top center, then sends them to `play.html?updating=1`, which waits for the new build and offers Enter System again. The length lives in `gamehttp.js` (`announceUpdate(part, 1)`) and the `sleep` in both deploy workflows.
- The deploy workflows are unchanged (`deploy-website.yml` on `website/**`, `deploy-server.yml` on `packages/server/**`).

- **Blueprints (owner):** clicking a slot gives a blueprint of that piece: a shimmering cyan hologram (bright edges, bands running up it, a sheen sweeping across, a faint flicker) that **follows the pointer**; **the wheel scales it** (both sides together, 5 m to 1,000 m; the camera doesn't zoom meanwhile); **holding R turns it slowly** (a quarter turn a second). ## Object Editor (owner, 2026-10-14; staff only)

- **[X]** (a button left of [B], or the X key) opens a window: the same objects as the Build menu (the shapes slot, which Q or a click cycles, and the test ship slot), drawn top down on a grid. Four tabs pick what's edited (owner, 2026-10-14: the shooting hitbox and what decides visibility are separate); all are always drawn (the one being edited bright, with handles):
  - **Aim Bounds** (was "Sight"; owner, 2026-10-14): the hitbox for aiming and shooting, and where the line of sight is stopped (line-of-sight shadows, the minimap, and where the aim line stops: owner, 2026-10-14). It starts as a 4-corner rectangle around the object (the shapes' square; the ship model's 59.4 × 100 m box). Drag a corner to move it; click the dot in the middle of an edge to add a corner there (and keep dragging it); right-click a corner to remove it (at least 3 stay). Concave is fine.
  - **Visibility** (owner, 2026-10-14): free points for the mutual visibility test. Click empty space to add one (Mirrored: a left/right pair, or one point on the middle line), drag to move (a middle-line point dragged sideways splits into a pair; a pair dragged together merges), right-click to remove (at least 1 stays; at most 32). They start at the box's 4 corners and 4 side middles. Reset puts them back.
  - **Movement:** where ships collide with it: an ellipse, starting as the footprint's own circle. Drag its middle to move it, a side handle to stretch it across or along.
  - **Pivot:** where it turns around (a crosshair to drag). A piece's position is its pivot; the model and both colliders sit around it.
- **The window:** drags around by its header; the wheel over the view zooms it in and out; zoomed in, the middle mouse drags the view around (owner, 2026-10-14). **Mirrored** (a checkbox, on when the game starts) keeps left and right matching: dragging a corner moves its mirror image too; a corner on the middle line stays on it until dragged clear sideways, when it splits into a mirrored pair, and a pair dragged together merges back into one corner on the middle line (owner, 2026-10-14), adding a corner adds its mirror, removing one removes its mirror, and the ellipse's middle and the pivot stay on the middle line. **Reset** puts the current one (Sight, Movement or Pivot) back to its start. **Ctrl+Z** undoes and **Ctrl+U** redoes (per object, while the window is open). **Rulers (owner, 2026-10-14):** a horizontal and a vertical dashed red line across the view, each dragged on its own; corners, the ellipse's handles and the pivot snap onto them when dragged within a few pixels.
- **In the world:** while the window is open, every piece's and every ship's colliders are drawn over everything: Sight outlines light blue, Movement ellipses orange, pivots as purple crosses.
- Shapes' colliders and pivot scale with each piece's size and turn with it.
- **Saved for everyone:** each change goes to the server (its own table, `world3d_colliders`: outline, pivot, ellipse per kind) and every piece of that kind takes it at once.
- **Ships:** the ship kind's three apply to **player ships** and test ships alike: a ship collides (with blocks, pieces and other ships) by its Movement ellipse turned with its heading (two ships each move half the overlap apart; the server decides, the client predicts the same); it casts no line-of-sight shadow (owner, 2026-10-14); its Sight outline still stops aim lines; its model turns around the pivot (the ship's position is the pivot). Its Sight outline stops other players' aim lines. The server checks every change: staff only, a known kind, 3-64 finite corners and finite numbers, clamped.
- Escape or × closes it.
