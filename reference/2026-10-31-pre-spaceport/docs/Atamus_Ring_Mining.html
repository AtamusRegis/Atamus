Crack, Bank
and Haul

How mining and ore work in Atamus. Asteroids are hidden masonry you survey and cut. Ore banks into the core and pays out only when the core pops. Then it's physical cargo you carry home, and your party shares all of it.

01The idea

Gathering together is the core of the game, so the cooperation lives inside the act of mining: how the rock is built, who maps it, who cuts where, when to pop it. It doesn't live in a payout table.

  • No PvP. No player can take ore you're carrying or ore that's locked to you.
  • Ore is physical. It goes into holds and cans and only becomes money when it's delivered. Your party shares everything.
  • Bore or strip. Boring is going straight for the core's ore without worrying about the ore around it. Stripping takes the whole rock, which pays far more.
  • No risk for now. Nothing in the world threatens ore in transit; the only thing that takes it is time.

This document covers mining and ore only. Crafting, station factories (solo or with a whole clan), missions, contracts, PvE and upgradable stations anyone can use are part of the full game and aren't covered here. The game is for PC; the prototype runs on a phone so it can be tested away from the computer.

02The asteroid

An asteroid is masonry around a core. It's built in shells of varying thickness. Each shell is made of chunks of varying length and thickness, with the seams staggered from one shell to the next, and thin chunks stack into towers. So the depth to the core changes with direction: two chunks one way, eight another.

  • Until it's scanned, an asteroid reads as one solid mass. No borders, no seams, no layout.
  • Lots of small chunks: up to about 100 on a large rock, and up to about 14 deep at a single angle.
  • Bore or strip. A tunnel reaches the core quickly for its ore. Every chunk still attached when the core pops is lost and shrinks the core's payout (see The core).

03Chunks

A chunk's health comes from its size: a long thick slab is a wall, and a thin sliver pops almost at once. Its density multiplies that.

  • About half of breakable chunks are barren: plain stone that breaks normally and holds no ore. Clearing it is real work, and it's what makes the colored veins feel valuable.
  • Bedrock is common, about one chunk in seven. The scanner spots it at once. The laser only finds out after cutting through every crack: the chunk holds, then turns dark red.
  • Bedrock ignores bursts.
  • Bedrock doesn't count when working out how stripped a rock is, so a fully worked rock still pays its full core around it.
  • Every rock keeps one straight line to the core free of bedrock, so a bore is always possible.

04Ore

  • Bigger asteroids hold more ore types: one on a small rock, two on a medium, three on a large.
  • Scanned chunks tint toward their ore, so a surveyed rock reads like a geology map: colored veins, grey overburden, red walls. Barren stone stays plain grey.
  • Ore comes in 1, 5 and 25 pieces. A payout fills the biggest pieces first, so a large haul is a few heavy pieces, not a hundred crumbs.
  • The core holds a separate rare ore, paid only when the core pops.

05Mining and scanning

Twin-stick controls. The left stick flies; the right stick aims and fires. On PC: WASD to fly, mouse to aim, hold to fire.

  • The miner's laser cuts the first solid chunk on its line. It can't reach through rock, so getting deeper means cutting your way there.
  • The scanner is its own ship. It reveals the one chunk its beam touches: its borders, density and ore type. That's all a scan shows.
  • Scanned chunks are see-through to the scanner, so holding one angle maps deeper and deeper, a chunk at a time, down to the core, without cutting anything.
  • Scanning is fast and cutting is slow. Mapping a chunk takes a fraction of a second; cutting one takes much longer.

Why the scanner matters. You can always mine blind. But with bedrock this common, a blind tunnel can run into a wall after you've spent all that cutting time on it. That wasted time is exactly what the scanner sells, and in a group one scanner's map is visible to everyone working the rock.

06Bursts

  • Breaking a chunk shocks every chunk touching it, side by side or stacked, for 25% of their maximum health.
  • Breaks cascade. A shock that finishes a chunk breaks it too, and that shocks its own neighbors. Chains rip through soft rock and thin stacked slabs, and dense chunks stop them.
  • The order you cut in matters. Working along a layer softens each next chunk before you reach it.
  • Bedrock and the core ignore shocks, so a chain can never pop a core by accident.

07The core

Broken chunks drop nothing. Their ore flies into the core and is banked there. Only the pop pays out.

  • The core swells as ore banks into it, up to half again its size, so a valuable rock is visible from across the field.
  • It ripens from gold to orange as it fills, and the more orange it is, the easier it is to break: up to four times faster when it holds all the ore the rock had. Patience makes the finale quick.
  • No numbers on screen. The core's size and glow are the only readout of what's banked.
  • The pop pays everything banked, plus the core's rare ore.
  • The rare payout shrinks for every chunk still attached. A fully stripped rock pays the full amount; a bore that leaves most of the rock standing pays a fraction.
  • Popping the core shatters everything still attached, and the ore in those chunks is lost.

Half-eaten rocks are worth finishing. Their ore is already banked in a fat, glowing core. Whoever pops it collects that bank, so abandoned work becomes something people want to pick up rather than litter.

  • Anyone can pop a core early, strangers included, even if it shatters what others were working on. That's accepted.
  • A partly mined rock lasts 30 minutes after it was last touched.

08Ships and roles

Each role is its own ship. Miners earn ore. Support is free inside a party, and players outside your party can charge credits for it.

Miner

Earns ore

Cuts rock with the laser. When the core pops, the ore goes into the miners' holds.

Scanner

Support

Maps rocks chunk by chunk: soft routes, ore veins, bedrock to avoid.

Booster

Support

Speeds up the mining of one player at a time, so the job is choosing who gets it.

Fueler

Support

Fuels other ships.

Hauler

Carries and refines ore

A big hold for party crates and unlocked cans, with a refinery aboard that compresses the cargo on the way home, so travel time becomes processing time. Can never drop what it picks up. Hauler and refiner are one ship for now.

  • Party members get each other's services for free.
  • Players outside your party can post a per-second charge for their service, used while they're nearby. Either side can stop it at any time, so nobody has to trust a stranger to pay or to keep working.

09Where ore goes

  • At the pop, ore goes straight into the miners' holds.
  • What doesn't fit goes into a can locked to that player. It stays private for 30 minutes, however far out it is, then becomes public for another 15 minutes, then disappears.
  • Public ore belongs to whoever delivers it.
  • One can per player at a time. A new can despawns your old one unless someone is already hauling it. Cans being hauled don't count toward the limit.
  • Unrelated miners on the same rock each get their own share of what they mined.

10Parties and crates

A party splits all its ore evenly on delivery, wherever its members are.

  • In a party, overflow cans are party crates, locked to the party. Anyone in the party can haul them.
  • The owner can unlock a crate for players outside the party, with a slider setting the share the deliverer keeps.
  • The share locks at pickup, so an owner can't post a high share to attract a hauler and then drop it once the crate is aboard. The slider runs from 5% to 50%.
  • Tags outlast the party. If the party breaks up before delivery, the split still goes to the members who were in it at pickup.
  • Your own ore isn't in a party crate, and it always has to be flown home.
  • No cargo status display. The game doesn't announce who is carrying what.

11Hauling and delivery

  • Haulers can never drop ore. Once something is picked up, the only way out is delivering it.
  • Delivery pays whoever the ore belongs to: the party split for party ore, the owner for their own ore, the deliverer for public ore.
  • Party crates auto-deliver at the daily downtime, every 24 hours, so a stalled shipment can't sit forever.
  • Except for ships logged off in the 30 minutes before downtime, so grabbing crates and logging off at the last minute doesn't skip the trip.
  • Your own ore never auto-delivers. Distance stays a real cost for the people mining.

Accepted risk. A hauler who stays online through the last half hour before each downtime can keep stalling a party's crates. The damage is limited to one hold, it can only come from someone the party chose to group with, and the party can deal with it socially.

12The prototype

A twin-stick phone build of everything in sections 02 to 07: one player, a Miner and a Scanner on a hotbar, and four asteroids on a field that respawn elsewhere when popped.

Open the prototype →

What it showed

  • Burst cascades were the biggest jump in how mining feels.
  • Densities gave rocks a grain worth reading.
  • Hidden layouts, scanning one chunk at a time and common bedrock made the scanner matter.

Not in it yet

  • A second player, so shared ore and the split can be felt
  • Holds, cans, crates and delivery
  • Boosting, fuel and support pay
